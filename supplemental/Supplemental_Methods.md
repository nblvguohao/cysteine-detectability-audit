# Supplemental Methods

This document gives the full experimental procedures behind the analyses summarized in Experimental Procedures of the main text: every setting, threshold, software version and data-handling decision. Reference numbers refer to the reference list of the main text. The registration status of every analysis is recorded in Supplemental Data 12.

### Sequences and structures
Protein sequences were taken from UniProtKB [29] by accession. Structures were the corresponding AlphaFold DB monomer models (version v6) [30,31], retrieved by accession. Proteins without a model were excluded and the exclusion was tested for bias against the retained set rather than assumed to be random.

### Detectability model
A residue was treated as theoretically detectable if at least one peptide of an in-silico digest contained it and had length 7–30 residues. Digestion was full cleavage with up to 2 missed cleavages; tryptic rules were cleavage C-terminal to K or R, suppressed before P. Where a second protease was evaluated, the same length and missed-cleavage settings were used and only the cleavage rule changed. This model is a sequence-combinatorial construct: it ignores cleavage efficiency, peptide ionization and identification rate, so figures derived from it are upper bounds. Every analysis states the window it used, and where none is stated the definition above is the one that applies. Three analyses add a mass or length criterion, and each is given with the analysis that uses it: the empirical check on the unenriched arm (at most 4,600 Da), the detectability baseline on the published benchmark (700 to 3,500 Da, Supplemental Note 5), and the detectability calibration of PXD048216, for which both a 7 to 30 and a 6 to 35 window are reported (Supplemental Note 7). That last analysis is the one place where two windows were computed on the same data, and it shows how much the choice moves: 13,254 predicted-observable cysteines with a positive predictive value of 26.58% under 7 to 30, against 14,110 and 25.11% under 6 to 35 (Supplemental Note 7).

### Per-residue structural features
Solvent accessibility was computed with the Shrake–Rupley algorithm [32] and expressed relative to the Gly-X-Gly tripeptide maximum of Tien et al. (2013) [33]; for cysteine the reference value is 167 Å². A residue was called fully buried at relative accessibility ≤ 0.05. In the structural claim re-tests, accessibility used the Shrake–Rupley implementation of biotite [35], and secondary structure was assigned from coordinates by P-SEA [34] as implemented there, which returns three states (helix, strand and coil). Model confidence was the mean per-atom B-factor of the residue. Local environment was counted within 8 Å of the sulfur atom: basic and acidic side-chain atoms, net charge, and other cysteines, the last of which is the neighboring-cysteine feature of Artifact 2. Disulfide bonds were assigned from SG–SG distance in the model (≤ 2.5 Å) and are predictions, not observations.

### Site-selection tests
All site-level tests of the artifacts used within-protein pairing: a modified residue was compared only against other residues of the same protein, so that protein-level properties (abundance, length, compartment, model quality) cannot contribute; the claim re-tests instead rebuild each author's own contrast, as described below. Backgrounds were, in increasing strictness, (a) all residues of the same type, (b) theoretically detectable residues, and (c) residues observed in the same experiment without a modification assignment. Every reported contrast states which background it used; (c) needs the identified cysteines of the same experiment and was used where a deposit or published cohort reports them (the four-protease deposit and the detectability-share analysis of main Fig. 2).

For continuous features, two versions of each test are reported: on raw values, and on within-protein normalized ranks. The rank version is a separate result rather than a robustness check; effects significant on raw values that do not survive it are labeled fragile rather than negative. For binary features, observed counts were compared against the within-protein expectation using a normal approximation; this is the z reported in Results. That approximation was validated by permutation on the polar STNQ class of the S-nitrosylation cohort, with 4,000 within-protein permutations: the permutation P of 0.7435 agreed with the analytic value. Because that class carries no effect, the approximation was also checked where an effect is present, by simulation at the parameters of the two cohorts whose binary statistics are reported here (Supplemental Note 13): over 25 simulated datasets per cell and 400 within-protein permutations per dataset, the analytic and permutation two-sided P values differed by a mean of 0.005 or less at the effect sizes of the reported statistics, |z| from 3.0 to 6.9, and by more than 0.05 in at most 4% of datasets, while under the null they differ by up to 0.02, where both values exceed 0.9 and no reading changes. Source Data carry full precision, and estimates are quoted here at the precision of the stored output. Multiple testing was corrected by Benjamini–Hochberg [36] across the full family of tests actually performed, not per feature or per dataset. Corrected values are recorded in the stored outputs; where the text quotes a P value it is the uncorrected one and is identified as such, and the self-audit tables of AUCs across five proteases and two label strata are descriptive and carry no multiplicity correction.

### Co-residue depletion and protease reach
Co-residue depletion was quantified by comparing the observed distribution of identified peptides by cysteine count against an in-silico tryptic digest of the same proteins, using the same length and missed-cleavage settings as the detectability model.

Protease reach was expressed as the fraction of cysteines for which some admissible peptide contains that cysteine and no other.

### Public cohorts, ranking and refitting
The self-audit uses two public persulfidation deposits: human THP-1 cells (PXD044043; PRIDE lists the organism as mouse, but both deposited MaxQuant searches used the human reference proteome and all twelve runs are THP-1 samples; Supplemental Note 2), with 18,567 cysteines in 1,519 proteins, 3,206 of them labeled, and rice (PXD072089), with 5,598 cysteines in 640 proteins, 817 of them labeled; for the zero-shot ranker, 594 rice cysteines in proteins absent from the reference proteome were excluded, leaving 5,004 cysteines, 737 labeled, in 581 proteins. Neither deposit's MaxQuant output contains a cysteine site table: a cysteine is labeled when it is the only cysteine of a peptide identified in the enriched arm, placed on that peptide's leading razor protein, and the rice cohort comprises every cysteine of the labeled proteins that carry at least two cysteines and have an active UniProtKB entry (release 2026_03) (Supplemental Notes 2, 19 and 21). The other cysteines belong to the same proteins, so an unlabeled cysteine is not known to be unmodified (a NEG_A negative in the tiers of the label-semantics table). For the first two self-audit analyses each cohort is scored once by the ranker trained on the other; the two cohorts are different species and share no UniProt accession. For the ablation and detectability-only analyses each arm was refit from scratch on each cohort by unchanged code, with five-fold cross-validation grouped by protein. That code fits three members per arm, a LightGBM ranking model, a LightGBM binary model and a small neural network, and blends them on the validation folds. The neural network receives, besides the arm's own columns, a 1,024-dimensional ProtBERT [55] embedding of each cysteine, the last-layer token of a 31-residue window centered on it, in raw and protein-centered form, so the blend of any arm carries language-model information that the arm's column list does not describe. Within-protein AUC is the mean of per-protein AUROCs over the proteins that carry both labels. Results in the text are read from the LightGBM ranking member, which sees only the arm's own columns; the registered reading was the blend, and both are reported. The binary member's label-permuted control fails on the human cohort, so that member is not read. The five structural columns are missing for external sequences, so absolute performance is not comparable across cohorts, only across arms within one. Folds and bootstrap clusters are proteins; a homology grouping links only 66 of the 1,519 human proteins, in 29 components, and none exists for rice, so intervals are protein-clustered bootstrap (5,000 replicates, fixed seed). The label-permuted control shuffles labels across proteins.

### Corpus of published site-preference claims
Claims were extracted from the primary literature of four cysteine switch chemistries (persulfidation, S-nitrosylation, sulfenylation, sulfinylation) into a single table, one row per claim, recording the authors' own statistic and sample size, the negative set the claim rests on, where in the paper or its supplement the site table lives, and whether the claim is re-testable with the instruments available here. Literature retrieval used relevance-ranked queries against Europe PMC; two known positive controls were declared before retrieval for each of persulfidation, S-nitrosylation and sulfenylation (none could be declared for sulfinylation), and their recall is reported per query. Where a control was not recalled, the corresponding family is reported as a candidate rather than as an absence.

Of the 51 claims, 14 were not re-testable at extraction: five had no defined negative set, six had their site table in a paywalled supplement and three had no site table. Of the 37 judged re-testable, four were not re-tested although their material was available: two pKa claims and one intrinsic-reactivity claim, for which no validated predictor was available, and one qualitative comparison between modifications that carries neither an author statistic nor a negative set of its own. Two of the paywalled claims, `SNO-\allowbreak{`012} and `SNO-\allowbreak{`014} [46], were added once their supplement was obtained, giving the 35 claims and 28 measured baselines reported in Results; of the other seven, six are outside the scope of the instrument and one remains blocked by unreachable material (Supplemental Data 1 and 2).

### Re-testing claims under detectability control
A claim was re-tested by rebuilding the authors' contrast on their own cohort and then adding the control the original analysis lacked. Two controls are applied and reported separately: a matched background, and a same-size random control drawn without matching. The matched background is built by propensity matching on a fixed covariate set rather than on covariates chosen per claim: every site-level claim uses the ten peptide-visibility covariates of Supplemental Note 5 computed under the tryptic rule (VIS10), and every protein-level claim uses eight protein-level aggregates of the same quantities (PROT8: length, the logarithm of the number of tryptic peptides, cysteine count, the number and fraction of cysteines in a detectable peptide, the number of detectable peptides, the mean hydropathy of cysteine-containing peptides, and the fraction of cysteines detectable when missed cleavages are allowed). Because VIS10 is computed from the positions of lysine and arginine around the cysteine, it is partly a function of any attribute defined by basic residues near the site; the consequence for those claims is quantified in Supplemental Note 15. The random control separates a loss of effect under control from a loss of power in a smaller sample, and it is reported for every matched contrast.

Intervals are cluster bootstrap percentile intervals over 5,000 replicates with the seed fixed in the script, clustering on protein or homology component rather than on site; splits and groupings isolate whole proteins or whole components. An interval that covers the null is reported as *undecidable*, not as *no effect*, and an interval lying entirely on the opposite side of the null is reported as an inverse association in its own category rather than as an absence. Where the published claim was itself a null, the reading is inverted relative to a directional claim: a preference that appears only once the control is applied counts against the null, whereas a preference already present in a reconstructed baseline that could not be rebuilt on the authors' own terms leaves the claim undecidable. Where a claim's attribute is binary and the reference is the label itself, the ceiling of the binary predictor is reported in Supplemental Note 9, read from the stored output rather than recomputed. It is a property of the label across all sites, and it is not the pooled closed form 0.5 + (rate_positive - rate_negative)/2, which gives 0.550 (rice) and 0.565 (human) for the distal trypsin band against the reported 0.5344 and 0.5558.

Verdicts are drawn from a fixed vocabulary: *survives*, *attenuated*, *undecidable*, *null_broken_by_control* (an effect that only appears once the control is applied), *baseline_contradicts_claim* (the reconstructed baseline runs against the published direction), *vanishes*, *out_of_instrument_scope*, and *blocked_material_unreachable*. Verdict tables are Supplemental Data 3, Supplemental Data 4, Supplemental Data 5 and Supplemental Data 6; the deduplicated tally is recomputed from those four tables rather than carried forward between rounds. Where a claim appears in more than one round, the tally takes its latest round and the earlier row is marked `superseded_by` in the table that carries it; where a claim was re-tested both on its authors' own data and in a transfer cohort, the tally takes the authors' own data and the transfer verdict is reported beside it (Supplemental Note 14).

Matching is checked before it is used. For `SFE-\allowbreak{`006}, the propensity AUC falls from 0.7616 to 0.5002, positives are retained at 0.9982 (1,093 of 1,095), and a within-group permutation is verified to be informative rather than degenerate (744 of 776 groups carry both classes; 18.05% of labels move). A round whose matching fails these pre-checks is reported as not re-testable rather than re-tested with a failed match.

### Detectability share under two negative-set constructions
For the detectability-share analysis (main Fig. 2), eight published site tables from tryptic bottom-up workflows, three for persulfidation, four for S-nitrosylation and one for sulfenylation (Supplemental Data 7), were each scored with two nested sets of features computed from the protein sequence under the tryptic rule. VIS10 comprises ten peptide-visibility features of the tryptic peptide carrying the cysteine: its length, log length, mass and hydropathy, and six detectability flags for the length and mass windows of that peptide and of its one- and two-missed-cleavage forms. DIG25 adds fifteen digest features: the position of the cysteine in its peptide (four columns, one of them a duplicate), the lengths of the missed-cleavage peptides (six columns), two protein-terminus flags, the number of tryptic peptides in the protein, the number of cysteines in the peptide and the number of lysines and arginines within 20 residues. Neither set contains chemical or structural features, although the lysine/arginine and cysteine counts may carry chemical information and two position columns determine peptide length exactly. One fixed gradient-boosted configuration was fitted without tuning, reweighting or calibration, with five-fold cross-validation grouped by homology component where a component table exists and by protein otherwise, to VIS10, to DIG25 and, as a third model, to the fifteen added columns alone. Each model was scored by out-of-fold AUC over all sites (global) and within proteins or components (pairwise concordance pooled over the units that carry both classes, reported when at least ten do). The ratio tabulated in Supplemental Note 20 is (AUC_VIS10 − 0.5)/(AUC_DIG25 − 0.5) on the global AUC: the fraction of the digest model's above-chance discrimination that its visibility subset reaches, not a partition of discrimination between visibility and chemistry. It is not reported when the denominator is below 0.02, and it is not truncated at one. Negatives were built in two ways for each cohort: NEG_A, every other cysteine of a protein carrying at least one site, and NEG_B, only the cysteines that the same experiment reported unmodified, which one cohort does not provide. Intervals are 95% percentile intervals from 5,000 cluster-bootstrap replicates (seed 20260915) in which both feature sets share each resample, so numerator and denominator are resampled together; models are not refit within replicates, and replicates in which the DIG25 AUC fell below 0.52 are left out of the ratio's interval, which affects one arm. Applying the tryptic rule to every cohort is an assumption of this analysis. The absolute AUCs, within-protein ratios and feature checks in Supplemental Note 20 were tabulated post hoc from the stored outputs.

### Diagnostic package and label semantics
These are described in Results and Supplemental Note 1. The two public training cohorts' sizes and the fitting procedure are recorded in Supplemental Note 2.

### Self-audit of the deployed ranker
Nothing was refit on the audited cohort in the first two self-audit analyses: the scores of the ranker trained on the other cohort were read, and the association between score and cleavage geometry was measured *within each label stratum*, against a permutation-distribution null (1,000 permutations of the scores within each stratum, whose 2.5th–97.5th percentile range must cover 0.5) and a within-protein permutation reported as the between-protein floor. The permutation-distribution null replaced a single-draw permutation before it was applied to any further cell, and the reverse direction (trained on rice, audited on human) was pre-registered with it before any rice-trained model existed. Cleavage rules for the five proteases respect cleavage side and proline blocking.

For the three-arm ablation, the verdict statistic, the branches for reading it, and the requirement that the label-permuted control produce no enrichment were fixed in the script's documentation before any arm was fitted. No paired difference test between arms was pre-specified; the one reported in Results is labeled post hoc.

### Planted-artifact benchmark
Four benchmarks were registered before the first dataset was drawn, with a fixed master seed, and are described in full in Supplemental Note 3: cleavage geometry, abundance-driven detection, multi-cysteine localization ambiguity and negative-class depletion. Each was generated at six injected strengths from zero to one with 50 replicate datasets per point, and a replicate counts as detected when its protein-clustered 95% bootstrap interval excludes that benchmark's null. The registered pass rule required a strength-zero flag rate of at most 0.1, sensitivity of at least 0.8 at strength one, AUROC of at least 0.75 and a rank correlation of at least 0.8 between strength and detection.

### Four-protease public deposit PXD063463
Each arm of this acyl-biotin exchange deposit was converted from its MaxQuant output: a cysteine counts as identified when it lies in a peptide with at least one evidence row in that arm, and as a site when it carries carbamidomethyl with a localization probability of at least 0.75, which is the readout of the exchange step in this chemistry. Arms processed without hydroxylamine were converted the same way and used as a specificity check. Each arm was audited with a cleavage rule derived from its own identified peptides and, for the non-tryptic arms, a second time with the tryptic rule. The cleavage-geometry statistic is the Haldane log2 odds ratio of carrying a cleavage-competent residue within 1 to 3 residues (proximal) or 6 to 12 residues (distal) of the cysteine, positives against background, with protein-clustered bootstrap intervals over 5,000 replicates at the Bonferroni level for the two bands; the registered comparison between rules uses the band with the larger absolute estimate. The proximal band is 1 to 3 residues in this analysis, in main Fig. 3 and in Fig. S1, and up to 5 residues in the self-audit tables of Supplemental Notes 9 and 10; every table states which band it uses. Backgrounds are every other cysteine of the identified proteins (proteome) or the identified cysteines without a site (observed). Coincidence is the share of identified cysteines carrying a site, with a protein-clustered 95% interval. Supplemental Note 4 gives every arm, band, background and count. As a post hoc addition, sites were also compared between the arms with and without hydroxylamine, and the cleavage-geometry statistic was recomputed against backgrounds matched on identification in the tryptic arm without hydroxylamine (post hoc; Supplemental Note 18).

### Empirical detectability check (PXD063463, unenriched arm)
A registered analysis asked how well the theoretical detectability rule predicts what an unenriched run actually identifies. Candidates were the cysteine-containing peptides of an in-silico Trypsin/P digest (cleavage after K or R, 0 to 2 missed cleavages, at least 7 residues, at most 4,600 Da) of the proteins identified in the deposit's global or acyl-biotin exchange arms, 264,782 candidates from 6,617 proteins, of which 6,658 were identified in the global arm's peptide table. The theoretical flag (length 7 to 30) was scored directly, and logistic models on standardized peptide features, protein abundance measured in the same run and identification in the other arm were evaluated by five-fold cross-validation grouped by protein. As a post hoc addition, backgrounds built from this arm were compared with the theoretical rule and with the observed background of the trypsin arm (post hoc; Supplemental Note 16).

### Protein-level abundance contrast
The unit is the protein group of the companion quantification, and a group counts as persulfidated when any of its accessions is a leading razor protein of the persulfidation peptide table. Abundance is iBAQ, taken from the first-listed accession of the group and summarized as the median over runs; raw intensity is a sensitivity arm (reason in Results). The abundance ratio is a ratio of medians with a bootstrap interval over protein groups (5,000 resamples, fixed seed); rice has no homology components here, so the interval is not homology-clustered and is narrower than a clustered one would be. Length and cysteine count are compared with a tie-corrected Mann-Whitney z before matching, within abundance deciles, combined by an unweighted Stouffer sum of the per-decile z (strata with fewer than ten in either arm dropped), and after one-to-one abundance matching; rank-biserial r is reported beside each z, on the 1,062 and 5,301 groups whose sequence was recovered. As a post hoc addition, the matched contrast was modeled on abundance, length and cysteine count, with null models calibrated to abundance and cysteine count (post hoc; Supplemental Note 19).

### The audited ranker
The audited ranker is a single LightGBM binary classifier, trained on one public PRIDE deposit by nested selection on that deposit's own protein-disjoint folds and applied zero-shot to the other, in both directions (PXD044043 to PXD072089, and PXD072089 to PXD044043; Supplemental Note 2); it is not the three-member blend that is refit within each cohort for the ablation and detectability-only analyses. Two feature sets appear in the self-audit: the chemistry set, which declares no visibility feature but includes basic-residue composition in windows out to 50 residues, and the full set, which adds the remaining members. The detectability-only set is 25 columns that answer only whether a peptide is identifiable; read through the LightGBM ranking member it contains no chemical, evolutionary or language-model information.

### Detectability-only baseline on a published benchmark
The pLMSNOSite training and test files were used as released (github.com/KCLabMTU/pLMSNOSite, commit e9158af, Apache-2.0 license; SHA-256 values in Supplemental Note 5). The paper reports 3,383 training sites and the released balanced training file has 3,276, so the released files were used and the published test-set AUROCs were quoted as published. Every listed position is a cysteine in the released sequence. The 25 detectability features and their ten-column visibility subset (peptide length, mass, hydropathy and the detectability windows, without position in the peptide or the local lysine and arginine count) were computed from the full sequence under the tryptic rule, and a gradient-boosted classifier with a fixed configuration was fitted once to the whole training set, without tuning or calibration. The test AUROC carries a 95% interval from 5,000 bootstrap resamples of test proteins, and the recovery ratio is (AUROC − 0.5)/(0.754 − 0.5), against pLMSNOSite. The question, data hashes, features, model, primary statistic, three reading branches and two controls (a label-permuted model whose interval had to cover 0.5, and five-fold cross-validation on the training set grouped by protein, mean AUROC 0.7653) were registered before any feature was computed; the registered branch for the 25-feature set required a recovery ratio of at least 0.75 and an interval above 0.5, and that branch was the one observed. As a post hoc addition, the released pLMSNOSite models were run on the released test set with the authors' own evaluation code (TensorFlow 2.15.1) to obtain score-based AUROC and AUPRC for the comparison (post hoc; Supplemental Note 22).

### Public deposit PXD048216
Artifact 4 was measured on PRIDE deposit PXD048216 from its MaxQuant `txt` output. Observed cysteines are the cysteines of peptides in `peptides.txt` on their leading razor protein, with reverse hits and contaminants removed; a site is the (protein, position) of a row of the carbamidomethyl or N-ethylmaleimide site table with localization probability at least 0.75, on that row's leading protein; per-replicate figures use the replicate-specific localization column. Sequences come from UniProt taxon 1159556; 1,429 of the 2,024 identified proteins are no longer in the current release and their sequences were recovered from UniSave, a historical version being accepted only if every identified peptide of the protein matches it at its stated start position (2,024 of 2,024 accepted). Intervals are Wilson intervals and protein-clustered bootstrap intervals (5,000 replicates, seed 20260921). The position-permutation control re-places each protein's carbamidomethyl sites at random among that protein's cysteines (200 permutations). The criteria were registered before the analysis; the headline fraction had been computed in an earlier exploratory pass. The 0.90 decision level was fixed after the two candidate statistics for this deposit had been inspected, so it is not a blind threshold. The modification-state composition of the multi-cysteine peptides is reported as a registered prediction, though the modifications column of the evidence table had been inspected before that prediction was written.

### Search space and mass accuracy
Sulfide (+31.972071 Da) and cysteine dioxidation (+31.989829 Da) differ by 0.017758 Da [41], which is 17,758/M ppm for a peptide of neutral mass M (Da). Three criteria are reported separately. The *window criterion* asks whether a search could have excluded the alternative: for an unbiased measurement the alternative lies outside a precursor window of pm t ppm only when M < M^* = 17,758/t Da, 1,776 Da at 10 ppm; with a systematic offset b (ppm) the limit becomes 17,758/(t+b) Da when sulfide is the true chemistry and 17,758/(t-b) Da when dioxidation is. The *resolvability rule*, registered before the re-search, calls a +32-class identification resolvable when its precursor error deviates from the file's own baseline, the median error of 1%-FDR identifications carrying no +32-class modification, by less than half the separation. The *mass-range criterion*, defined post hoc, gives the largest peptide mass for which the errors expected under the two chemistries lie at least four baseline standard deviations apart, M le 17,758/(4 SD) Da.

The five raw files of PRIDE deposit PXD015307 [42,43] were re-searched: Fig-1D (498,563,222 bytes), the proteome-scale enrichment, and four files, Fig4-L_NaHS, Fig4-L_CTH_Cys, Fig4-L_heat_inactive_CTH and Fig4-L_control (99,612,094 to 150,754,884 bytes), in which the deposit's own identifications are insulin chains only. Raw files were converted to mzML with ThermoRawFileParser 2.0.0.dev (bioconda build h9ee0642_1) [44] and searched with Comet [45] 2026.01 rev. 1 (e4f767c) against UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot *Mus musculus* (reference proteome UP000000589, canonical sequences and isoforms, release 2026_03, downloaded on 17 September 2026, 25,750 entries), the species of the deposit's own search; for the four insulin files bovine insulin (P01317) was appended. No contaminant sequences were appended. Decoys were generated by Comet as reversed peptide sequences with the C-terminal residue retained and searched concatenated with the targets. Trypsin was specified as fully specific (cleavage after K or R, not before P) with up to two missed cleavages. The precursor tolerance was ±10 ppm in every file, all of whose survey scans are FTMS, without isotope-error correction. Fragment settings followed each file's MS2 analyzer: a 0.02-Da fragment bin for Fig-1D (FTMS MS2) and, for the four insulin files (ion-trap MS2), a 1.0005-Da bin with a 0.4 offset, scoring only the theoretical fragment peak. No static modification was set. Variable modifications were sulfide, dioxidation, iodoTMT6plex (+329.226595 Da) and carbamidomethyl (+57.021464 Da) on cysteine and oxidation (+15.9949 Da) on methionine, at most three per peptide; protein N-terminal acetylation and N-ethylmaleimide were not searched. Five candidates were reported per spectrum. Rank-1 PSMs were filtered per file at 1% PSM-level FDR [86], estimated as the ratio of decoy to target PSMs ranked by Comet E-value, without rescoring and without peptide- or protein-level control. Every setting, its source record, the per-file counts and the output files with their checksums are listed in Supplemental Note 6.

The question, the resolvability rule and the outcome branches, including the branch taken (with fewer than twenty +32-class identifications at 1% FDR the round is reported as underpowered and no rate is quoted), were registered before any search was run. After earlier runs, four of which failed on configuration, the database species and the fragment settings were amended and bovine insulin was appended (Supplemental Note 6). The readings of the deposit's own consensus files, of score ties and fragment binning, and of the mass range each criterion covers are post hoc.

The phosphorylation/sulfation extension applies the window criterion with the same code and one added table entry (HPO_3 versus SO_3, 0.009515 Da from element masses) to eight deposits returned by one registered PRIDE keyword query (“phosphoproteome”), taken in API order. Two exceeded a 200-MB combined-file cap and were not fetched, and two had not searched phosphorylation. For the remaining four, the modification list and the main-search tolerance were read from each deposit's own mqpar.xml (for PXD071110 also its Andromeda .apar files), since the parameters.txt files state neither. This check uses the tolerance, the modification list and the peptide masses only; it uses no fragment-level evidence and no measured precursor errors.

### Software
Analyses were run in Python with biopython [49], biotite [35], numpy [50], openpyxl, scikit-learn [51], scipy [52] and statsmodels [53], with the versions of the pinned environments listed below; LightGBM [48] is used for the three-arm ablation and the self-audit, and the figures were drawn with matplotlib 3.9.4 [54] under Python 3.9.6. Three pinned environments were used: Python 3.11.16 with numpy 2.4.6, scipy 1.17.1, scikit-learn 1.9.1 and biotite 1.6.0 for rounds A and B of the claim re-tests and for the structural site features; Python 3.8.10 with numpy 1.24.4, LightGBM 4.6.0 and PyTorch 2.3.1 for the three-arm ablation and the detectability-only refits (the ProtBERT embeddings they read were computed once, with PyTorch 2.11.0 and transformers 4.57.2); and Python 3.9.6 with numpy 2.0.2, scikit-learn 1.6.1 and LightGBM 4.6.0 for the later re-test rounds (the `SFE-\allowbreak{`006} own-data re-run and rounds D and E) and for all other model fitting. The post hoc analyses were run in Python 3.10.11 with numpy 2.2.6, pandas 2.3.3, scipy 1.15.3, scikit-learn 1.7.2, statsmodels 0.14.6, LightGBM 4.7.0 and biopython 1.88, against UniProt release 2026_03, and the released pLMSNOSite models under TensorFlow 2.15.1. Values are compared only within an environment, because the same propensity model can give slightly different values across environments (for example −0.6735 versus −0.6765 for claim `SNO-\allowbreak{`016} [7] and −0.0158 versus −0.0317 for `SFE-\allowbreak{`008} [6]).

### Literature survey
Eight queries (four per source) were run against Europe PMC and OpenAlex (articles, 2005-2026), each a product of modification-type terms and site-feature or motif terms. Records were deduplicated on DOI and title. Titles and abstracts were screened by Claude (Anthropic) against a written rubric; the false-negative rate of this screen was not estimated. Full text was retrieved from Europe PMC as XML, and a fixed-seed random sample was coded against the same rubric by Claude from regex-extracted passages, with two verification passes over the full-text XML and a verbatim quotation recorded as the evidence for each code; papers without retrievable full text were never coded from abstracts. Proportions carry Wilson score 95% intervals.

**Coding reliability.** The 74 classified records were re-coded from the retained evidence in two further passes, each applied to an extract that withheld the original code and carried only the title, the modification, the study type and the recorded evidence quotations, and each coding every record with a recorded justification. Agreement with the original codes was 46 of 74 and 37 of 74 (Cohen's kappa 0.31 and 0.31, treating “unclassifiable from the retained evidence” as its own category), and 57 of 74 and 48 of 74 (kappa 0.58 and 0.46) if the twelve records for which a background statement was found in the full text on re-grep but not retained are credited to the original code. All passes used the same language model, so these figures bound how far the retained evidence determines the code rather than measuring agreement between human coders, and the false-negative rate of the screen remains unestimated. Two limitations are reported rather than repaired: 51 of the 74 retained background quotations are cut off mid-clause, in several cases immediately before the clause that names the background, and 12 records carry a note that a background statement exists in the full text although the statement itself was not retained, so the class cannot be recovered from the released table alone. Disagreement concentrated on records whose retained quotation is cut off before the clause that names the background (22 of 28 disagreements in one pass, 32 of 37 in the other), and, in the remainder, on records whose retained evidence describes a negative set that is curated, substrate-restricted or drawn from an external database rather than detected in the same experiment. The class assigned to every record by each pass, and the reason for each disagreement, are released as Supplemental Data 11, so the count of matched backgrounds can be recomputed record by record. Of the five records on which the original count rested, both passes reproduced the original class for two and disagreed for three; the three were then re-read against the full text and confirmed to describe a negative set that is curated, substrate-restricted or drawn from an external database, which is why the count reported here is two.

The eight queries returned 3,134 records, 2,250 after deduplication. Title and abstract screening retained 942 that appeared to perform site-level feature or motif analysis. Full text was retrievable for 367 of these (39.0%); of a fixed-seed random sample of 120, 45 proved on full text not to be site-level feature analyses and one had an empty body, leaving the 74 classifiable papers reported in Results.

### Experimental Design and Statistical Rationale
This study generated no new mass spectrometry data, so the sample-size, replicate-type, randomization, standard, MS1-acquisition, fractionation and spectral-library items of the journal's proteomic checklist do not apply to it. The analyses re-use public deposits, published site tables and papers, or synthetic datasets with planted artifacts, listed under Data Availability. The unit of replication is the protein, or the homology component where one exists, not the site: splits, folds and bootstrap clusters isolate whole proteins, because the sites of one protein share its abundance and sequence context. Unless stated otherwise, intervals are 95% percentile intervals from 5,000 cluster-bootstrap replicates with the seed fixed in the script, and an interval that covers the null is reported as undecidable rather than as no effect. Site-level tests are corrected by Benjamini–Hochberg across the full family of tests performed (Site-selection tests). Controls are built into the comparisons: a same-size random control beside each matched contrast, label-permuted models for the model-based analyses, and planted artifacts of known size for the audit statistics. The analysis plans and reading rules for the PXD048216 analysis, the planted-artifact benchmark, the four-protease deposit and the published-benchmark baseline were registered before those analyses ran (Supplemental Notes 3–5 and 7). For the PXD015307 re-search, the question, reading rule and outcome branches were registered before any search was run, and the database species and fragment settings were amended after runs that failed on configuration (Supplemental Note 6). Throughout, “registered” means an internal pre-specification: the plan and reading rule were written into a protocol file that is written before the analysis ran and released with its SHA-256 value in the code repository (folder `protocols`); none of these plans was lodged with an external registry, and the public history of the repository begins with the release that accompanies the manuscript, so the order of registration and analysis rests on these internal records rather than on public commit timestamps. The analyses added after the primary analyses (Supplemental Notes 14–22) are post hoc and are labeled as such. In simulation with realistic protein clusters, the protein-clustered percentile interval used throughout held close to its nominal 5% type-I error, from 0.050 with 1,475 proteins to 0.066 with 44 (Supplemental Note 17). Supplemental Data 12 records, for every analysis in the paper, whether it was registered, which artifact carries the registration, whether its reading rule was fixed before the run, and which decisions were taken after a result had been seen.

Every analysis round records the sha256 of the script that produced it inside its audit record, and thresholds, reading rules and negative-set definitions are written into that script's documentation before the run; anything decided afterwards is labeled post hoc in the text. Corrections are appended rather than substituted, so the original number, the reason and the corrected value are all retained in the stored audit records. Assertions of the form “all”, “none”, “only”, or any count spanning result sets, are computed from the complete stored tables rather than from prose. Negative and fragile results are reported in the same place and at the same length as positive ones. A count or absence obtained from partial or capped input is reported as not assessed at that depth, never as an absence.
