# Supplemental Note 14. The claim re-tests: final tally and what the matched control changes (post hoc)

**Status.** Post hoc: added after the primary analyses were complete. This Note gives the final tally of the 28 published claims re-tested against a measured baseline, the estimates behind each verdict, the re-run of `SFE-006` on its authors' own data and a comparison, by attribute class, of how much of each effect matching removes. The class comparison was defined after the verdicts had been seen. No re-test was re-estimated for this Note: every value is read from the stored re-test outputs, and retained fractions and shifts are arithmetic on stored point estimates.

**How the verdicts are read.** Each re-test rebuilds the claimed contrast (the reconstructed baseline) and estimates it again after propensity matching on a fixed covariate set, VIS10 for site-level claims and PROT8 for protein-level claims, beside a same-size random control drawn without matching (Experimental Procedures). Estimates are log2 odds ratios with 95% cluster-bootstrap percentile intervals (5,000 replicates, clustered on protein), not adjusted for multiplicity across the re-tests.
- *Directional claims.* A baseline interval that excludes zero on the side opposite the claim is recorded as *baseline contradicts claim*, and one that covers zero as *undecidable*, because there is no baseline effect to control. Otherwise the matched estimate is read against the baseline: *survives* if its interval excludes zero with the baseline's sign and it keeps at least half of the baseline log2 odds ratio; *attenuated* if it keeps less than half; *reverses* if its interval excludes zero on the other side; *vanishes* if its interval covers zero while the random control's does not; *undecidable* if both cover zero. If a Mantel–Haenszel estimate stratified on quintiles of the same propensity score falls in a different class, *survives* is recorded as *attenuated* and *vanishes* as *undecidable*.
- *Null claims*, under the reading declared before they were re-tested: a baseline interval that covers zero followed by a matched interval that excludes it is *null broken by control*; a baseline interval that already excludes zero is recorded as *undecidable*.
- The branch for a baseline interval that covers zero changes a stored verdict only for `SNO-012`, the one directional claim whose baseline interval covers zero but whose matched interval does not; Supplemental Data 6 stores it as attenuated, from pipeline code that lacked this branch (notes to Table S14.1).

**Tally.** Thirty-five distinct claims entered the re-test; 28 have a measured baseline, 18 site-level and 10 protein-level, and the other seven are listed in Supplemental Data 2 and enter no count. The tally is that of Supplemental Data 2 with two changes. `SFE-006` was re-tested both in a transfer cohort and on its authors' own data; it is tallied on its authors' data, where it is attenuated, and its transfer verdict, vanishes, is reported beside the tally, because a transfer re-test is not evidence either way about the authors' cohort. The same principle, tallying each claim on the material closest to its authors' own analysis, places `PERS-008` against the background of its authors' main-text enrichment (below). `SNO-012`, stored as attenuated, is undecidable because its baseline interval covers zero (above).

No directional claim reverses, and no tallied claim vanishes; the effect of `SFE-006` vanishes only in its transfer re-test. Of the two null claims, `SNO-004` acquires a preference only after matching and `PERS-002` is undecidable. The one baseline that runs against the claimed direction (`SNO-016`) was rebuilt in a transfer cohort. Both attenuated verdicts are losses of more than half of a baseline effect under matching (`PERS-009`, `SFE-006`). In 11 of the 13 undecidable claims the reconstructed baseline interval already covered zero before any control was applied; the other two are `PERS-002`, a null claim whose baseline already excludes zero, and `SFE-008`, a transfer re-test whose matched and random-control intervals both cover zero. The seven protein-level survivors are not read as support: without a protein-level negative class, which none of these cohorts provides, protein-level commonality is not estimable (Table 1, item 6).

**Notes to Table S14.1.**
- `PERS-002`: null claim. Its reconstructed baseline is already a deficit, which matching leaves in place, and the null-claim reading records a baseline that excludes zero as undecidable. The deficit is compatible with its authors' wording that such motifs are not preferential targets, though not with the absence of any preference.
- `SNO-004`: null claim (modified cysteines equally distributed between hydrophobic and hydrophilic areas). The preference appears only after matching and not in the same-size random control. It is marginal (matched lower bound 0.062), and because VIS10 includes the hydropathy of the tryptic peptide that carries the window, part of the shift may be built into the control.
- `SFE-006`: re-run on the Yang 2014 component of its authors' positive set (next section). Its transfer re-test, in an Arabidopsis sulfenylation cohort (9,777 cysteines, 1,745 positives; Supplemental Data 4), gives a baseline of 1.16 [0.96, 1.36], a matched estimate of 0.18 [−0.081, 0.44] and a random control of 1.18 [0.90, 1.47]: *vanishes*, reported here and not tallied.
- `SNO-012`: the baseline interval covers zero, so the claim is undecidable under the stated rule, as in the released Cys-Audit tool. Supplemental Data 6 stores it as attenuated, from pipeline code that lacked this branch and read the matched estimate directly. Matching reveals an exposure preference (1.75 [0.53, 3.22]) that stratification on the same propensity score does not (0.58 [−0.32, 1.55]). With pLDDT ≥ 70 the baseline is 0.89 [0.048, 1.81] and the claim survives (Supplemental Data 6), so its verdict depends on the specification.
- `SNO-016`: transfer cohort (human S-nitrosylation); the reconstructed baseline runs against the claimed direction.
- `PERS-008`: against the zinc-finger complement, the background of its authors' main-text enrichment (474 proteins, 118 positives); the whole-proteome reading is given below.
- `PERS-010`: log2 of observed over expected commonality, the claim's own estimand (inset of Fig. S4), not a log2 odds ratio.


**Tool version and the `SFE-006` random control.** In Cys-Audit 0.2.0 the random control collapsed onto the baseline under stratification; this was corrected in 0.2.1, and all 18 site-level claims were re-run under the released version (Supplemental Data 12, row `Claims_A_B_D_E`).

**`SFE-006` on its authors' own data.** The positive set of `SFE-006` (Bui et al. 2016) is the union of the sulfenylation sites of Yang et al. (2014) with RedoxDB: 1,443 positives and 10,521 negatives in 987 proteins. The re-run uses the Yang 2014 component. Positives are taken from the Total sheet of Yang 2014 Supplementary Dataset 2 and mapped by peptide onto the human reference proteome; negatives are all other cysteines of the same proteins, as the authors' rule specifies; the attribute offsets are those of the transfer re-test, unchanged. The cohort has 9,240 sites, 1,095 positives and 776 proteins. The baseline log2 odds ratio is 1.38 [1.11, 1.67] (Fisher P = 3.85 × 10⁻²⁹, uncorrected). Matching on VIS10 retained 1,093 of the 1,095 positives and lowered the propensity AUC from 0.762 to 0.500; the matched log2 odds ratio is 0.59 [0.27, 0.93], 0.43 of the baseline, while the same-size random control (1,093 negatives, 20 draws) gives 1.39 [1.03, 1.78], 1.01 of the baseline. Stratification on quintiles of the same propensity score gives 0.57 [0.27, 0.87] and the same verdict. On its authors' data the effect is therefore attenuated, not absent, and the loss comes from matching rather than from the smaller sample. The re-run covers only the Yang 2014 component (1,095 positives, against 1,443 in the published union).

**What the control changes, by attribute class.** The retained fraction, the matched log2 odds ratio over the baseline one, is defined where the reconstructed baseline showed the claimed effect, that is, where its interval excluded zero in the claimed direction. On the authors' own data this holds for 6 of the 15 site-level claims (Table S14.2). Matching kept 0.43, 0.37 and 0.55 of the baseline for the three basic-residue motifs (`SFE-006`, `PERS-009`, `SNO-021`), 0.83 and 1.02 for the two acidic motifs (`SFE-002`, `SNO-006`) and 0.89 for the one structural attribute (`SFE-001`). The same-size random control kept 0.91 to 1.02 in every class, so the basic-motif losses come from matching, not from the smaller matched samples. The absolute shifts order the basic and acidic motifs the same way (−0.79 to −0.47 against −0.14 to +0.025); the structural shift, −0.29, lies between them, and its ratio is high partly because its baseline, 2.57, is the largest in the set. The contrast also holds within one cohort: the `SFE-006` re-run and the `SFE-002` re-test were both run on the Yang 2014 sites and store identical cohort fields (9,240 sites, 1,095 positives, 776 proteins, 1,093 matched pairs, propensity AUC 0.762 before and 0.500 after matching), and there lysine or arginine at the `SFE-006` offsets kept 0.43 while glutamate at the `SFE-002` offsets kept 0.83. This is the direction that tryptic cleavage geometry predicts (Artifact 1). Because the matching covariates are computed from the positions of lysine and arginine around the cysteine, however, the contrast cannot by itself separate a detectability artifact from genuine lysine/arginine-dependent chemistry (Supplemental Note 15), and it rests on three basic against two acidic claims.

**Codings checked against the source papers.** Three codings follow what their source papers state. `SNO-012` is read in the direction its authors report, a preference for sulfur-atom exposure beyond their 1.0 Å² burial criterion (they report a slight overall enrichment for exposure); its verdicts are in Table S14.1 and its notes. `SNO-002` is re-tested under its stated threshold, relative accessibility above 10%, normalized to cysteine in Ala-Cys-Ala; it is undecidable under both codings, in all five stored specifications. `PERS-008` is tallied against the zinc-finger complement that its authors used as the background of their main-text enrichment (474 proteins, 118 positives), where it is undecidable. As a sensitivity analysis, against the whole proteome (54,855 proteins), the background of their supplementary analysis, its baseline log2 odds ratio is 3.50 [2.55, 4.18] and the matched estimate 1.93 [0.54, 3.98], 0.55 of the baseline, and it survives; with that reading, 12 claims would survive and 12 be undecidable (protein level 8 and 2). Whether either protein universe matches the authors' background exactly was not checked. The first codings (`SNO-012` as a null, `SNO-002` at a borrowed 25% threshold, `PERS-008` against the whole proteome) were changed after their results had been seen; the rows they produced remain in Supplemental Data 5 and 6.
