# Supplemental Note 15. Does the matched background remove genuine chemistry? (post hoc)

**Status.** Post hoc: added after the primary analyses were complete. The simulation, its chemistry and detection models, the attributes and the calibration rules were all chosen after the primary results had been seen; the calibration rules were fixed before the runs they select, but after earlier simulation grids had been seen. Every statement is limited to the processes simulated, and all values are read or recomputed from stored outputs (Data and code, below).

**Question.** The claim re-tests compare the positives of a site table with a background of the other cysteines of the same proteins, matched to the positives on a propensity score built from ten peptide-visibility features (VIS10; Experimental Procedures and Supplemental Note 5). VIS10 omits the number of lysines and arginines within 20 residues of the cysteine, because such a count can itself be genuine chemistry, but it contains the length, mass and detectability windows of the cysteine's tryptic peptide, and these are set by where the flanking lysines and arginines lie. Matching on VIS10 could therefore remove genuine chemistry of a basic-residue attribute together with a detectability artifact. Published claims carry no known truth, and the planted-artifact benchmark (Supplemental Note 3) plants artifacts only, so this Note plants genuine effects in simulated site tables and measures how much of them matching removes.

**How the simulation was built.**
- *Real sequences and real detection.* Sequences are those of the mouse reference proteome (UniProt UP000000589, release 2026_03). A cysteine counts as detected when one of its candidate tryptic peptides (the in-silico digest of the empirical detectability check; Experimental Procedures) was identified, at the same protein, start and sequence, in the unenriched (global) arm of PXD063463. Of the 264,782 candidate peptides, 97.7% matched the 2026_03 sequence at their stated start; the 139 proteins absent from that release or carrying any mismatch were dropped.
- *Universe.* The 28,471 cysteines of the 2,445 proteins that carry at least one detected cysteine; 6,870 of them (24.1%) are detected.
- *Site tables.* Positives are drawn among detected cysteines at an expected 20%, and the background is every other cysteine of the proteins that carry a positive, as in the re-tests. A table holds about 1,370 positives and 13,200 sites from 970 proteins (observed detection, no chemistry).
- *Re-test code, unchanged.* Each table is analyzed with the re-test code of this paper, imported unchanged: a logistic propensity model of the label on standardized VIS10, 1:1 nearest-neighbor matching on its logit without replacement within a caliper of 0.2 standard deviations of the logit, and the Haldane-corrected log2 odds ratio before matching (baseline) and after it (matched). Retention is matched over baseline.
- *Attributes.* The six re-tested attributes of Table S15.1, computed with the attribute functions of the re-tests, and a sequence-independent attribute assigned to a random 30% of cysteines, redrawn in every table.
- *Genuine chemistry.* Among detected cysteines, the odds of being a positive rise either (a) with the claim's attribute as the claim defines it, by a fixed log2 odds ratio (0.5, 1.0 and 1.5 for the loss estimates; 0 to 3.0 in steps of 0.25 for calibration), or (b) with the local lysine/arginine count, the number of K and R within ±20 residues (the count VIS10 omits; standardized over the universe, SD 2.2 residues), by b log2 odds ratio per SD (0 to 4 in steps of 0.5, then 5 and 6). Without chemistry and under observed detection (below), positives are a random subset of the detected cysteines.
- *Detection models.* Observed detection, as above: positives are drawn only among the cysteines actually identified, with no further selection. A steeper selection, in which positives are in addition weighted toward cysteines whose tryptic peptide has a high predicted detectability (a peptide-level detection model fitted to the same arm); it raises the propensity AUC of a table without chemistry from 0.67 to 0.72. Permuted detection, in which the identified cysteines are permuted within each protein, so that detection carries no sequence information and any loss under matching is over-adjustment.
- *Loss beyond a sequence-independent attribute.* Matching shrinks even a sequence-independent effect, to 0.88–0.89 of the planted log2 odds ratio under observed detection, because the matched background still contains undetected cysteines. The loss specific to an attribute is therefore measured against that reference: the part of the planted effect that survives matching (the matched log2 odds ratio with chemistry minus that without) is divided by the planted log2 odds ratio, or by the rise it caused in the baseline, and then by the same quantity for the sequence-independent attribute in the same tables. The loss is one minus this ratio, reported as the range over both normalizations and the three planted effects.
- *Calibration to the observed re-tests.* For each claim, chemistry model and detection model, the strength whose mean simulated baseline equals the observed baseline was found by linear interpolation along the grid, and the observed retention was compared with the retention per simulated table (median [2.5th, 97.5th percentile] over tables). For `SFE-006`, 200 tables were simulated at the interpolated strength itself; for the other claims, the nearest grid strength is used.
- *Replication.* 100 to 500 simulated tables per cell, with fixed seeds.

**VIS10 partly encodes basic-residue attributes.** On the universe, a logistic regression on VIS10 predicts detection with out-of-fold AUC 0.706 (five-fold cross-validation, folds by protein). The same procedure predicts the four re-tested attributes that involve lysine or arginine with AUC 0.66–0.84, and the two acidic ones much less well, 0.59–0.61 (Table S15.1). A linear model on VIS10 predicts the omitted count within ±20 residues with out-of-fold R² 0.44.

**Genuine effects on the attribute as the claim defines it.** Under observed detection, matching removed 3–11% of a genuine effect planted on a claim's named residue offsets (`SFE-006`, `SNO-021`) and 12–23% of one planted on a five-residue composition (`SNO-016`, `PERS-009`), beyond what it removed from the sequence-independent attribute; for the acidic attributes the figure was 3–12%. Table S15.1 gives each attribute under all three detection models. Had the observed baselines come from such an effect, matching would have kept 0.77 [0.62, 0.89] of the `SFE-006` baseline under observed detection (0.70 [0.55, 0.82] under the steeper selection and 0.82 [0.69, 0.94] under permuted detection), and 0.98 [0.83, 1.12] and 1.08 [0.93, 1.22] of the `SNO-021` and `PERS-009` baselines (observed detection). The observed retentions, 0.43, 0.55 and 0.37, lie below the 2.5th percentile of these ranges in all three detection models, so over-adjustment of chemistry that acts on the attribute as defined does not account for the attenuation of these three claims.

**Chemistry that depends on the local lysine/arginine count.** The count within ±20 residues correlates with the four attributes that involve lysine or arginine (Pearson r 0.25–0.39 over the universe) and not with the acidic ones (|r| ≤ 0.02). Chemistry that follows the count therefore raises the baselines of those four attributes, and because VIS10 partly encodes the count, matching removes much of that rise. At the strength that reproduces the observed `SFE-006` baseline (2.71, 3.17 and 2.38 log2 odds ratio per SD under observed detection, the steeper selection and permuted detection), the `SFE-006` attribute kept 0.47 [0.29, 0.58], 0.45 [0.31, 0.60] and 0.37 [0.17, 0.53] of its baseline, against 0.43 observed. Only for `SFE-006` are both the observed baseline and the observed retention reproduced in all three detection models. For `SNO-021`, no simulated strength reached the observed baseline, although at the strongest strength simulated its retention (0.45–0.61 across the three models) spans the observed 0.55. For `PERS-009`, this chemistry reached the observed baseline only under permuted detection, where the attribute kept 0.25 [0.06, 0.42] against 0.37 observed. It raised the acidic baselines by at most 0.11 log2 odds ratio and reproduces neither acidic claim.

**Reading.** Within the processes simulated:
1. A genuine effect that acts on a claim's attribute as the claim defines it loses only 3–11% (named offsets) or 12–23% (compositions) to matching beyond what a sequence-independent attribute loses, under observed detection.
2. For claims about basic residues near the site, the matched control, which is built to remove detectability artifacts, cannot by itself separate them from chemistry that depends on the local lysine/arginine count: it removes such chemistry about as much as it removed the `SFE-006` association (retention 0.37–0.47 in simulation, 0.43 observed). An attenuated verdict for such a claim records what the control removes; it is not evidence that the association was an artifact. The contrast between attenuated basic-residue claims and retained acidic ones (Supplemental Note 14) does not separate the two either: genuine acidic effects lose 3–12% to matching beyond the sequence-independent attribute (observed detection), and count-dependent chemistry raised the acidic baselines by at most 0.11 log2 odds ratio, so the contrast is expected whether the basic-residue associations are detectability artifacts or such chemistry.
3. Nor do the simulations show that the `SFE-006` association is chemistry. Selecting positives among detected cysteines by the length of their tryptic peptide, with no chemistry at all, reproduced the observed `SFE-006` baseline (mean 1.381 log2 odds ratio over 300 tables, against 1.384), although matching then removed more of it than in the re-test (median retention 0.11 [−0.15, 0.28], against 0.43).
4. The reproduction of the observed loss by count-dependent chemistry is full only for `SFE-006`; it is partial for `SNO-021` (retention, not baseline) and holds for `PERS-009` only when detection carries no sequence information.

**Limits.**
1. Detection is that of the unenriched arm of one mouse macrophage deposit: real, but a stand-in for the unknown detection processes behind the published cohorts, whose propensity AUCs (0.73–0.76 in the five re-tests with an observed retention in Table S15.1) are higher than those of simulated tables without chemistry (0.67 under observed detection, 0.72 under the steeper selection).
2. Each chemistry model is a single term acting on the log odds among detected cysteines. Chemistry that also changes detectability, and structural chemistry linked to VIS10 through peptide hydropathy, were not simulated.
3. The count-dependent model follows the K/R count within ±20 residues. Chemistry following the K/R count within ±5 or the A/K/R/V count within ±10, simulated under observed detection only, also reproduced the observed `SFE-006` baseline (at 6.09 and 4.52 log2 odds ratio per SD) and kept 0.32 [0.18, 0.43] and 0.56 [0.42, 0.67] of it, on either side of the observed 0.43; it reproduced the `PERS-009` baseline as well, but was then retained (0.94 [0.78, 1.12] and 0.89 [0.65, 1.07]) rather than removed.
4. Simulated tables hold about 1,370 positives, against 982 (`SNO-021`), 1,095 (`SFE-006`) and 2,701 (`PERS-009`) in the re-tests compared. At a positive rate matched to the `SFE-006` cohort (about 1,100 positives per table) and at the same strengths, the `SFE-006` attribute kept 0.45 [0.28, 0.59] under count-dependent chemistry and 0.77 [0.60, 0.92] under chemistry on the attribute (observed detection).
5. Only the site-level background, VIS10 and the matching of the re-tests were simulated; protein-level claims, the 25-feature digest set (DIG25) and `SNO-014` (56 positives) were not.
