# Supplemental Note 17. Calibration of the protein-clustered percentile interval (post hoc)

**Status.** Added in revision, post hoc, in response to review. The analysis was designed after the review, with the stored claim verdicts known, and was not pre-specified. It is a sensitivity analysis and changes no stored verdict. All values are read from stored outputs or, for Table S17.1, pooled from the stored per-cell results by counting (Data and code, below).

**The interval.** Each claim re-test verdict depends on whether 95% percentile intervals of a Haldane-corrected log2 odds ratio (0.5 added to every cell of the 2 × 2 table) exclude zero. The intervals come from 5,000 bootstrap replicates that resample whole proteins (or homology components), the construction used for the paper's intervals generally. This Note reports how often such an interval excludes zero when there is no association, whether the stored verdicts depend on its small miscalibration, and how many of the stored exclusions multiplicity could account for.

**Simulation.** Cohorts of 44, 164, 776 and 1,475 proteins were simulated; 44 proteins is the size of the smallest re-test cohort (`SNO-012`), and 164 that of the cohort behind six S-nitrosylation re-tests. The number of cysteines per protein was 1 plus a negative binomial count (size 2) or a heavier-tailed discretized log-normal count (log-scale SD 0.94), both with a mean of 8.5, close to the 8.3 to 9.0 cysteines per protein of the smallest re-test cohorts. In each dataset about 15% of cysteines were positive and an attribute was present on about 15% or 35%. Label and attribute varied either between cysteines only or also between proteins, through protein random intercepts with SD 1 on the logit scale that were independent of each other. The two prevalences, two cluster-size distributions and two random-effect settings give eight cells per cohort size, simulated with 4,000 datasets per cell at 44 proteins, 3,000 at 164 and 1,000 at 776 and 1,475: 72,000 datasets without any association. Coverage was estimated in as many datasets with protein random intercepts and a within-protein effect that set the pooled log2 odds ratio to 0.5 or 1.0.

Each dataset was analyzed as the whole cohort, as a reconstructed baseline is, and as all positives plus as many randomly drawn negatives, the size of a matched set. The percentile interval was computed by the released Cys-Audit function. For comparison, the bias-corrected and accelerated (BCa) interval [82] was computed from the same replicates, with the acceleration from a leave-one-protein-out jackknife, and a cluster-robust Wald interval around the same estimate used the standard error of a logistic generalized estimating equation with independence working correlation and the bias-corrected covariance of Mancl and DeRouen [83], with t quantiles on G − 1 degrees of freedom for G proteins. Seeds are fixed in the scripts.

**Reading.** Without association, the paper's interval excluded zero in 0.066 of datasets with 44 proteins (Monte Carlo SE 0.001), 0.056 with 164 (0.001), 0.052 with 776 (0.002) and 0.050 with 1,475 (0.002), and it covered the pooled log2 odds ratio in 93% to 95% of datasets. It is slightly anti-conservative only in the smaller cohorts: with 44 proteins its half-widths would need to be about 7% longer for nominal coverage, with 164 about 3%, and from 776 proteins on it is calibrated. At 44 proteins, BCa gave 0.057 and the Mancl–DeRouen interval 0.045; from 164 proteins on, all three methods lay between 0.049 and 0.056. At the size of a matched set the percentile interval gave similar rates, 0.050 to 0.065.

**Registered cleavage benchmark.** The cleavage benchmark of Supplemental Note 3 reads the same kind of interval, a Haldane log2 odds ratio with a protein-clustered percentile interval from 5,000 replicates, on about 300 synthetic proteins per dataset. With its generator functions taken verbatim from the benchmark script and its strength-zero path re-implemented, the 50 stored strength-zero replicates of the registered run were reproduced exactly (estimates and bounds differ by at most 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁶; 7 flagged), as were the 21 flags among the 300 replicates of the extension run outside the registration. Run with new seeds on 10,000 further strength-zero datasets, the same generator flagged 513, a rate of 0.051 (Wilson 95% interval 0.047 to 0.056); the factor by which half-widths would need to be multiplied for nominal coverage was 1.00 [0.99, 1.02]. Because the stored replicates are draws from this generator, the registered run's 7 of 50 (0.14) and the extension's 21 of 300 (0.070) were chance excesses over a calibrated rate, not a miscalibration of the interval. The abundance benchmark, whose statistic is a rank AUC, was not re-simulated. Source: `revision_2026-09-30/results/E_bootstrap_coverage/r1_b1_null.json`.

**Stored verdicts.** The re-test rule, with its functions taken verbatim from the pipeline's source and the steps that combine them re-implemented, reproduces all 28 stored verdicts from the stored intervals (for `SFE-006`, its transfer re-test; its re-test on the authors' own data was not part of this check). Each stored percentile interval was then rescaled about its point estimate by the half-width factor simulated at its claim's cohort shape, in claim-matched null simulations (2,000 datasets per configuration, copying each cohort's protein count, mean cluster size, positives and attribute prevalence) where available and in the simulation above otherwise; random-control intervals were rescaled against the simulated interval of one same-size random draw. Verdicts were re-derived at the central factor and at both ends of its simulated range.
- 26 of the 28 verdicts are the same at every reading, and none changes at every reading.
- `SFE-008`, a transfer re-test stored as undecidable, reads vanishes at the central factor and at one end of the range and undecidable at the other; the bound that decides it is the lower bound of its random-control interval, −0.070.
- `PERS-003`, stored as undecidable, reads attenuated at the low end of its range; the bound that decides it, the matched lower bound of −0.015, is also 2.0 Monte Carlo standard deviations of the bootstrap from zero.
- No claim re-tested on its authors' own data reads vanishes at any reading.

Source: `revision_2026-09-30/results/E_bootstrap_coverage/claims_table_check.json`, `.../claims_matched_null_configs.csv`, `.../r1_calibrated_verdicts.csv` and `.../r1_summary.json`.

**Multiplicity.** The re-test intervals are not adjusted for multiplicity. Of the 84 baseline, matched and random-control intervals of the 28 stored re-tests, 47 exclude zero; about 4 (84 × 0.05) would be expected by chance if no association existed. Because the bootstrap replicates of the stored re-tests were not kept, each interval's P value was approximated from its stored bounds by a normal approximation on the log2 scale. Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment at a false discovery rate of 0.05 across the 84 intervals, the procedure the paper applies to its site-level tests, retains 46 of the 47 exclusions. The one it does not retain is the matched interval of `SNO-004`, the one null claim contradicted under the control (0.73 [0.06, 1.42]; P ≈ 0.032), so that contradiction does not survive the adjustment. The adjustment moves the threshold, not the estimate, so it shows that the contradiction rests on one marginal bound, not that the null holds. Source: `revision_2026-09-30/results/E_bootstrap_coverage/multiplicity_intervals.csv`, `.../multiplicity_summary.json` and `.../multiplicity_verdicts.csv`.

**Limits.** The simulation has one binary attribute and logit-normal protein effects that are independent between label and attribute, with no structure within proteins. The matched set is represented by random negatives, so dependence created by propensity matching is not simulated. The P values of the stored intervals are normal approximations from two bounds.
