# Supplemental Note 18. Hydroxylamine-dependent sites and identification-matched backgrounds in the four-protease deposit PXD063463 (post hoc)

**Status.** Added in revision, post hoc, in response to review. Every definition, control and stratification in this Note was chosen after the Figure 2 results had been seen. Cleavage statistics were computed with Cys-Audit v0.2.2 on the arm tables filtered to UniProt release 2026_03, with the settings of the Figure 2 audits: the Haldane log2 odds ratio of carrying a cleavage-competent residue 1 to 3 (proximal) or 6 to 12 (distal) residues from the cysteine, sites against background, with protein-clustered bootstrap intervals over 5,000 replicates at the Bonferroni level for the two bands (97.5%; Supplemental Note 4). Their counts are therefore slightly smaller than elsewhere in this Note (for example, 1,354 rather than 1,359 trypsin-arm sites). All other counts and shares use the arm tables as audited for Figure 2; with the release-filtered tables, the shares and differences reported here agree within 0.002. Other intervals are protein-clustered bootstrap intervals (95%, 5,000 replicates), paired where two arms or two rules are compared. Seeds are fixed in the scripts. Intervals are per comparison, without correction across the many definitions, strata and controls, and are read descriptively: an interval that covers zero is undecidable, not evidence of no association.

**Figure 2 with a current sequence release.** Figure 2 was audited with the sequence database distributed with the deposit. Re-running its eleven audits with UniProt release 2026_03 required dropping 43 rows of the arms treated with hydroxylamine (35 trypsin, 4 chymotrypsin and 4 GluC, 8 of them sites) whose accessions the release no longer contains; no row pointed at a residue other than cysteine. Over the 22 band estimates, the largest change was 0.026 log2 units (median 0.005) and the largest change of an interval bound 0.051. Coincidences changed by at most 0.002, the AspN arm, which lost no row, reproduced exactly, and the archived v0.1.0 of the tool gave the same statistics as v0.2.2. The upper bound of the GluC distal band against the observed background lies close to zero: it is 0.044 with the deposit's database and −0.007 with release 2026_03, and with the new release the interval covers zero in 46 of 50 bootstrap seeds. No distal interval against the observed background lies above zero with either release or with any of these seeds.

**Candidate hydroxylamine-dependent sites.** A site is a cysteine that carries the carbamidomethyl readout (localization probability at least 0.75) in an arm treated with hydroxylamine; a cysteine is identified in an arm when it lies in a peptide with at least one evidence row there (Supplemental Note 4). Each site was classified by the arms processed without hydroxylamine: *strict*, its cysteine was identified there without the readout; *lenient*, it did not carry the readout there, whether identified or not; *readout in both*, it carried the readout there too. The arms without hydroxylamine were used matched to the protease and, assuming that each enrichment was labeled before it was split into digests, pooled across the four proteases; the tryptic arm contributes 95.5% of the pooled identifications. These definitions score presence or absence. Without intensities, which the converted tables do not carry, none of them measures hydroxylamine dependence.

Where an arm without hydroxylamine identified a site's cysteine, it usually carried the readout there as well: for 538 of the 708 such trypsin-arm sites (0.76 [0.72, 0.79]), for 0.72 to 0.73 of such chymotrypsin and GluC sites, and for 3 of 6 AspN sites. Scored as presence or absence, the readout therefore occurs without hydroxylamine at most of the sites that can be checked, and its presence alone does not establish hydroxylamine dependence. The checkable sites are a detectability-selected subset: the tryptic arm without hydroxylamine identified 2.6 times fewer cysteines than the arm with it and has 25.5% of them in the top decile of unenriched protein intensity, against 13.7%. The non-tryptic arms without hydroxylamine identified only 22 to 68 cysteines, so their matched lenient sets retain 94 to 97% of the sites, mostly for lack of evidence.

**The readout with and without hydroxylamine.** For the 1,767 cysteines that both tryptic arms identified, the readout is more frequent in the arm with hydroxylamine: 0.40 against 0.35 (difference without minus with −0.050 [−0.071, −0.030]), with 170 cysteines carrying it only with hydroxylamine and 81 only without (discordant cysteines; exact two-sided binomial P = 2.0 × 10⁻⁸). The smaller arms point the same way (3 against 0, 7 against 1 and 12 against 1 discordant cysteines among the 8, 41 and 57 that the AspN, chymotrypsin and GluC arms identified both with and without hydroxylamine). The excess is concentrated in proteins of which the arm with hydroxylamine identified more cysteines (134 against 55 discordant cysteines; 36 against 26 in proteins where it identified as many or fewer). Because a cysteine counts as carrying the readout when any of its evidence rows does, deeper coverage could contribute to this excess; hydroxylamine-dependent enrichment would itself deepen coverage, so these tables cannot separate the two. The shares of all identified cysteines, 0.33 without hydroxylamine (660 of 2,024) and 0.26 with it (1,359 of 5,328), are not directly comparable, because the arms differ in depth: standardizing the arm with hydroxylamine to the protein-abundance distribution of the other reduces the difference from +0.071 [0.048, 0.095] to +0.027 [0.008, 0.048], and standardizing the other way reduces it to +0.007 [−0.015, 0.028] (Table S18.2).

**Cleavage geometry against backgrounds matched on identification.** Identification in the tryptic arm without hydroxylamine selects cysteines by their own tryptic detectability. It raises the share of cysteines with K or R 6 to 12 residues away in every class of cysteine, whether or not it is a site (sites / identified non-sites / unidentified cysteines, inside against outside that stratum: AspN 0.82 / 0.92 / 0.85 against 0.72 / 0.74 / 0.73; chymotrypsin 0.86 / 0.93 / 0.88 against 0.75 / 0.75 / 0.73; GluC 0.85 / 0.86 / 0.88 against 0.77 / 0.79 / 0.74). Sites split by this identification, or selected on it, were therefore compared with background cysteines of the same stratum, identified or not identified in that arm, under the proteome and observed definitions of Supplemental Note 4.

In every non-tryptic arm the own-rule distal signature persists in both strata, among the sites that the tryptic arm without hydroxylamine identified and among the rest: AspN 1.96 [0.82, 3.56] and 1.13 [0.20, 2.38], chymotrypsin 1.45 [0.81, 2.17] and 1.26 [0.76, 1.85], GluC 1.13 [0.49, 1.85] and 0.90 [0.46, 1.37] (Table S18.3). Under the trypsin rule the estimates are −0.36 to 0.25, and no trypsin-rule interval excludes zero (upper bounds 0.67 to 1.14). The difference between the rules, estimated on shared bootstrap resamples, excludes zero at the 95% level in all six strata; at 97.5%, the interval for AspN sites outside the stratum reaches −0.09. The signature therefore follows the protease used within both strata, not only in the stored sets. With identification in any arm without hydroxylamine as the stratum, the own-rule distal signature again persists in both strata (1.93, 1.54 and 1.12 inside; 1.08, 1.20 and 0.88 outside, for AspN, chymotrypsin and GluC), against trypsin-rule estimates of −0.88 to 0.37.

**Coincidence between digests under depth controls.** The share of identified cysteines carrying a site is 0.26 [0.24, 0.27] in the trypsin arm against 0.61, 0.56 and 0.59 in the AspN, chymotrypsin and GluC arms (Fig. 2c), which identified 6 to 28 times fewer cysteines. The difference from trypsin persists when the trypsin arm is standardized to each other arm's distribution of unenriched protein intensity (0.26 to 0.27; the trypsin share rises only to 0.30 to 0.34) and when both arms are restricted to the proteins they share (0.20 to 0.27; 0.12 to 0.21 with equal protein weights). It is smaller for the same cysteines: 0.12 [0.05, 0.18] for chymotrypsin and 0.13 [0.07, 0.18] for GluC, and 0.05 [−0.05, 0.15] for AspN, whose interval includes zero (Table S18.5). In each of the ten abundance deciles, every non-tryptic arm has the higher share (top decile: 0.50 for trypsin against 0.73 to 0.75); AspN is lower only among cysteines of proteins without an intensity value (0.19 against 0.23; 27 cysteines). A protein-level coverage proxy does not explain the same-cysteine differences: on the proteins it shares with each arm, the trypsin arm identified 3.5, 2.9 and 2.4 times more cysteines, yet carried the readout less often on the same cysteines, and the share of discordant cysteines carrying it only in the trypsin arm shows no trend across tertiles of this coverage ratio (AspN 0.42, 0.40, 0.42; chymotrypsin 0.33, 0.30, 0.32; GluC 0.30, 0.37, 0.23). The contrast therefore lies mainly in which cysteines each digest identifies, which depth at the peptide level, not measurable in these tables, may also shape.

**Limits.** The converted tables carry neither intensities nor per-cysteine evidence counts, so every definition here scores presence or absence, and no comparison measures hydroxylamine dependence or specificity. Strict sites are selected on identification in an arm without hydroxylamine, and lenient sites treat absence of evidence as absence of the readout. Comparisons within an identification stratum condition on a variable that depends on cleavage geometry, abundance and other peptide properties, so they can be biased in either direction, and an interval that covers zero within a stratum is absence of evidence, not proof of absence. Pooling the arms without hydroxylamine assumes that each enrichment was labeled before it was split into digests, which could not be checked. Protein intensity measures cellular abundance rather than abundance in the enriched sample, the coverage proxy is protein-level, and the same-cysteine comparisons condition on identification in both arms. The deposit is one S-acylation study in one organism.

**Data and code.** All values are read from stored outputs under `revision_2026-09-30/results/C_pxd063463_specific/`, whose `provenance.json` records the sha256 of the pipeline's inputs, scripts and outputs. The pipeline is `revision_2026-09-30/scripts/C_pxd063463_specific/run_all.py`. The differences between rules in Table S18.3 were computed by `verify_r3_d_rule_difference.py` in the same folder, an independent re-implementation used for internal verification, with one protein-clustered bootstrap resample shared by both rules (inputs, environment and seeds in `verify_r3/provenance_verify_r3_d_rule_difference.json`); its point estimates equal the differences between the pipeline's estimates.
