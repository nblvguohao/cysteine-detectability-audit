# Supplemental Note 21. How the self-audit labels were assigned, and the reference level of the top-100 statistic (post hoc)

**Status.** Added in revision, post hoc, in response to review; no reading rule was fixed before these analyses ran. Values were computed from the released site table of the two public cohorts, the deposits' own files, UniProt sequences (release 2026_03) and the stored outputs behind Supplemental Notes 9 and 11. No model was refitted. Unless stated otherwise, intervals are 95% protein-clustered bootstrap intervals (5,000 replicates).

**How the labels were assigned.** Neither deposit's MaxQuant output contains a cysteine site table. The two MaxQuant outputs of PXD044043, for the enriched and the input arm, hold site tables only for methionine oxidation; carbamidomethylation of cysteine was a fixed modification, and no cysteine modification was variable. The only processed file of PXD072089 is a MaxQuant peptide table whose only site-level column is also for methionine oxidation. The labels of both cohorts are therefore our own mapping of the peptides identified in the enriched arm onto UniProt sequences. A cysteine is labeled when it is the only cysteine of an identified peptide, placed on that peptide's leading razor protein, and a cohort comprises every cysteine of the proteins that carry at least one label and at least two cysteines. For rice, this rule reproduces the released labels site by site from the deposit's peptide table: the 817 labeled cysteines are exactly the cohort cysteines that are the only cysteine of an identified peptide on its leading razor protein, none missing and none extra, and all 97 cohort cysteines identified only in peptides with two or more cysteines are unlabeled. Supplemental Note 19 gives the full rice chain, including the loss of proteins whose UniProt entries had been deleted. For human, the deposit's peptide tables were not re-read. The rule is inferred from the released 15-residue sequence windows (next paragraph) and from our project records, which describe the positives of both cohorts as inferred from single-cysteine peptides; whether the human mapping also used the leading razor protein could not be checked.

**Cysteines that can never be labeled.** Every fully tryptic peptide that contains a cysteine contains the cysteine's minimal tryptic peptide, the stretch between the nearest lysine or arginine on either side. A cysteine whose minimal tryptic peptide holds a second cysteine can therefore never be the only cysteine of a tryptic peptide: no single-cysteine peptide can cover it, and it cannot be labeled. The rice search allowed cleavage before proline (44 identified peptides end at a lysine or arginine that precedes a proline, and 59 begin with a proline that follows a lysine or arginine), so the minimal peptide is taken with cleavage after every lysine and arginine. On full UniProt sequences, which match all 5,598 rice cysteines, none of the 817 labeled rice cysteines shares its minimal tryptic peptide with another cysteine, against 1,709 of the 4,781 unlabeled cysteines (35.7%, in 384 of the 640 proteins). Full human sequences were not available for this check, so it was made within the released 15-residue windows, which can miss a second cysteine outside the window: none of the 3,206 labeled human cysteines, against 4,002 of the 15,361 unlabeled ones (26.1%, in 802 of the 1,519 proteins; 4,038 when cleavage before proline is blocked). The human 26.1% is therefore a lower bound; in rice, the same windows find 1,160 of the 1,709. The window holds the whole minimal peptide about equally often in the two human classes (1,030 of 3,206 labeled and 4,839 of 15,361 unlabeled cysteines, 32.1% and 31.5%), and there the check is exact: none of the 1,030 labeled cysteines against 695 of the 4,839 unlabeled ones (14.4%).

**The ranker's features can read this rule.** The chemistry feature set (1,046 columns), used by the zero-shot ranker and by the chemistry-set arm of the refit, and both deletion arms carry 48 columns that describe neighboring cysteines, with 44 within-protein transforms of them: distances to the nearest other cysteine, counts of other cysteines within 4 to 64 residues, cysteine counts and fractions in windows of 1 to 50 residues, and indicators of a cysteine at each position from −6 to +6. The detectability-only and full feature sets also carry the number of cysteines in the cysteine's own tryptic peptide. In rice, on full sequences, single columns separate the cysteines that can never be labeled from all other cysteines with these AUCs: distance to the nearest other cysteine, 0.916 [0.906, 0.926]; number of other cysteines within 10 residues, 0.841 [0.827, 0.856]; number within 4 residues, 0.733 [0.716, 0.750]; and number of cysteines in the tryptic peptide, 0.992 (point estimate; the count is two or more for every such cysteine). These AUCs were not computed for human.

**What this means for the self-audit.** A ranker trained on these labels can learn part of the labeling rule, so part of its ranking can reflect how the labels were assigned rather than cysteine chemistry. On its own, whether a cysteine can be labeled at all separates labeled from unlabeled cysteines within proteins (the mean of per-protein AUCs, as in Table 2) with AUC 0.602 [0.595, 0.608] in human (a lower bound from the windows; 1,475 proteins) and 0.642 [0.632, 0.653] in rice (full sequences, all 5,598 cysteines; 637 proteins). In the zero-shot self-audit (Supplemental Notes 9 and 10; Figure 7a, d), the label-positive stratum contains no cysteine that the rule excludes (for human, as far as the windows show), so the rule cannot produce ordering there by this route. The label-negative stratum mixes the two kinds. In rice, the unlabeled cysteines that can never be labeled carry the distal band, a trypsin cleavage site 6 to 12 residues from the cysteine, less often than the other unlabeled cysteines, 0.680 [0.649, 0.709] against 0.777 [0.762, 0.792]. A score that encoded only this property would therefore order the 4,781 unlabeled rice cysteines by the distal band with AUC 0.558 [0.540, 0.576] (0.561 [0.541, 0.581] on the 3,764 whose protein is in the 2026_03 reference proteome), against the observed 0.6474 for the chemistry set and 0.6457 for the full set on the 4,267 unlabeled cysteines analyzed. Whether the fitted models rely on these columns was not tested. The labeling rule is a property of the annotation workflow, not of cysteine chemistry, and its effect is consistent with our reading of the self-audit as a measure of annotation ranking rather than chemical propensity (Results).

**The reference level of the top-100 statistic.** The verdict statistic of the ablation (Supplemental Note 11; Table 2) is the Haldane-corrected log2 odds ratio of a trypsin cleavage site 6 to 12 residues from the cysteine among the 100 top-scored cysteines of a cohort, against all other cysteines. It is computed on all 18,567 human cysteines, 14,670 of which carry such a site, and on the 5,004 rice cysteines whose protein is in the reference proteome file used, 3,803 of which carry one. Each stored value is fixed by a single count, the number of the 100 that carry the site: from that count and the cohort totals, all 24 stored values of Supplemental Note 11 are reproduced exactly at their stored precision. The registered reading compared each interval with zero, and all six ablation intervals exclude zero (Supplemental Note 11). Zero is the level expected when the top 100 are a random draw from the cohort. Labeled cysteines, however, carry the site more often than unlabeled ones (0.897 against 0.768 in human and 0.845 against 0.745 in rice; Supplemental Note 9), partly because of the labeling rule: in rice, the cysteines that can never be labeled account for 31% [21%, 45%] of the labeled–unlabeled difference in the rate of the distal band (all 5,598 cysteines; not computed for human). A score that places labeled cysteines first, with no information about cleavage geometry, therefore produces a positive value. The reference for this statistic is the level that label inheritance alone produces, not zero: a top 100 of labeled cysteines chosen without regard to cleavage geometry gives an expected 89.7 cysteines with the site in human and 84.5 in rice, which correspond to log2 odds ratios of 1.16 and 0.76 (Table S21.1).

In human, every reading after the named columns are deleted lies at or above the label-inheritance range. Without the win7/win10 basic-composition columns, 95 of the 100 top-scored cysteines carry the site under both readings, the upper limit of the range (P = 0.046); without all twenty basic-composition columns, 97 carry it under the ranking member (P = 0.0058) and 99 under the registered blend (P = 0.00021), both above the range. All four post-deletion values, 2.2132 to 4.1488, exceed the label-inheritance level of 1.16. In rice, the ranking-member readings lie above or at the upper limit of the range (95, 93 and 91) and the registered-blend readings lie within it (84, 86 and 85). These comparisons are descriptive: the P values are unadjusted for the twelve readings and treat each observed count as fixed, and the stored interval of every reading except one (human, registered blend, minus all twenty columns) overlaps the reference range.

**Within-protein AUC after deleting all twenty basic-composition columns.** Read through the ranking member, deleting all twenty basic-composition columns reduced the within-protein AUC for the label in human by 0.009: the paired difference, deletion arm minus chemistry-set arm, is −0.00901 [−0.01703, −0.00047] (post hoc paired protein-clustered bootstrap; Supplemental Note 11). It is computed on all 18,567 human cysteines, over the 1,475 proteins that carry both labeled and unlabeled cysteines; the other 44 proteins have every cysteine labeled. The registered blend gives −0.01189 [−0.01937, −0.00442] on the same proteins. In rice, the ranking member's paired difference, +0.01108 [−0.00395, 0.02632], was computed over 637 proteins, the number that carry both classes among all 5,598 cysteines on which the arms were trained, whereas the rice rows of Table 2 and of the arm tables of Supplemental Note 11 use the 5,004 cysteines whose protein is in the reference proteome file used (578 proteins that carry both classes).
