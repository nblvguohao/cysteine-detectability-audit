## SR1. Artifact 1: tryptic cleavage geometry (full text)

Main text: the section on tryptic cleavage geometry; Fig. 3 and Fig. S1.

Comparing residues flanking modified cysteines with those flanking all other cysteines of the same proteins gave a strong enrichment of lysine, arginine and histidine in three *Arabidopsis thaliana* site sets, the cysteine entries of the PlantPTMViewer [56,57] categories for S-nitrosylation, S-sulfenylation and reversible cysteine oxidation: z = +15.72, +10.03 and +5.59. Its positional profile is not that of a local chemical environment. Basic residues are *depleted* next to the site and enriched five to seven residues away: in an Arabidopsis S-nitrosylation site table (the FAT-switch cohort; Supplemental Data 7), the K/R fraction at position −1 is 0.4576 [0.3681, 0.5487] of the proteome background, against 1.4975 [1.3572, 1.6421] at +5, 1.4630 [1.3226, 1.6084] at +6 and 1.3831 [1.2417, 1.5235] at +7. This cohort is not one of the three PlantPTMViewer sets above. This is the geometry trypsin imposes: a detected peptide almost always ends in K or R, so an observed cysteine sits a few residues from a cleavage site.

Restricting the background to theoretically detectable cysteines removed most of the signal for reversible oxidation (+5.59 → +0.67, P = 0.50 uncorrected) and attenuated it in the other two sets (+15.72 → +8.22; +10.03 → +4.71), whereas an acidic-residue signal was unchanged or slightly stronger (+6.04 → +6.62 for reversible oxidation), so the correction does not attenuate every composition feature. Six of the eight datasets analyzed in this study were documented trypsin or Lys-C/trypsin digests, and the tryptic rule was assumed for the other two (Supplemental Data 8).

The direct test is a non-tryptic digest of the same enrichment. PRIDE [28] held no proteome-scale non-tryptic deposit for persulfidation, S-nitrosylation or sulfenylation, so we used an S-acylation deposit in which one ABE enrichment was digested with four proteases (PXD063463 [37]). Scored with each arm's own cleavage rule, the distal band carries the signature in every arm (log2 odds ratio 1.5391 [0.7930, 2.4927] for AspN, 1.1946 [0.8020, 1.6302] for chymotrypsin, 0.9449 [0.5739, 1.3475] for GluC and 0.9730 [0.7115, 1.2756] for trypsin); scored with the trypsin rule, the non-tryptic arms give 0.1189 [−0.5549, 0.8637], 0.3349 [−0.0656, 0.7750] and 0.3840 [−0.0640, 0.8466] (Fig. S1a). The signature follows the protease that was used. These intervals admit a modest residual, and in the proximal band the chymotrypsin arm keeps 0.4011 [0.0572, 0.7400] under the trypsin rule, so the tryptic association is strongly reduced rather than shown to be absent. Against the deposit's own observed background, the identified cysteines that carry no assignment, no arm shows a distal signature (trypsin 0.0608 [−0.2353, 0.3808]; Fig. S1b). Three arms are small (187, 728 and 884 identified cysteines against 5,328 for trypsin), so the comparison between rules rests mainly on the trypsin arm (Supplemental Note 4); matching sites and background on identification in the tryptic arm processed without hydroxylamine leaves the own-rule distal signature in every non-tryptic arm and no trypsin-rule interval above zero (post hoc; Supplemental Note 18).

## SR2. Artifact 2: asymmetric filtering of multi-cysteine peptides (full text)

Main text: the section on asymmetric filtering and abundance. The seven-dataset values are stored in the repository file `artifacts12/results/35_symmetric_ncys_test.json`; the script that produced the symmetric estimates was not retained (Supplemental Methods, Data provenance).

When a site table is derived from peptide-level reports without per-residue localization, peptides with more than one cysteine are ambiguous, and retaining them places every cysteine they contain in the positive set, enriching the positives for cysteine-dense neighborhoods by construction. For the neighboring-cysteine feature, another cysteine within 8 Å of the sulfur, restricting only the positives to single-cysteine peptides while the background remains all detectable cysteines gives z = −2.61 (P = 0.009 uncorrected, 756 sites) in the rice persulfidome (PXD072089 [39]), whereas restricting both sets gives z = +3.34 (689 sites); in the human persulfidome (PXD044043 [40]) the values are −8.78 and +2.67. Across all seven datasets compared in this way (these two, the three Arabidopsis site sets of Artifact 1, and pooled human and mouse S-nitrosylation and S-glutathionylation sites from dbPTM [58]), the asymmetric filter gave the more negative estimate in every case, from −0.98 to −11.09, while the symmetric estimate was positive in four and negative in three, from +4.53 to −3.64. The asymmetry moves every dataset in one direction, whereas the symmetric value is a property of the dataset, so it is the asymmetry of the filter, not the presence of multi-cysteine peptides, that reverses the feature. **The remedy must be symmetric:** either both sets are filtered or the feature is not reported. The values of the two peptide-inferred cohorts above therefore demonstrate the filter and are not biological estimates; tables with instrument-assigned localization are unaffected.

## SR3. Artifact 3: abundance matching and the protein-level contrast (full text)

Main text: the section on asymmetric filtering and abundance; Supplemental Note 19.

Asking whether modified *proteins* share properties requires a background of unmodified proteins, and protein detection in an enrichment is strongly abundance-dependent. In the rice persulfidome and the total proteome acquired alongside it (PXD072089 and PXD072035 [39]), persulfidated protein groups were 10.36-fold more abundant at the median than the other quantified groups (95% CI 8.95 to 12.29; 1,197 versus 6,495 groups), or 4.88-fold on raw intensity, which rises with protein length, one of the properties tested.

Matching on abundance did not remove a protein-level separation; it exposed one. On the 1,062 modified and 5,301 unmodified groups whose sequence was recovered, protein length did not separate the two sets when abundance was ignored (rank-biserial −0.009) but did within abundance deciles (+0.201), and cysteine count moved from +0.104 to +0.290. One-to-one abundance matching, which retains the fewest proteins, gave +0.170 and +0.256, and the direction held under three definitions of the modified set and on the 479 groups defined by single-cysteine peptides alone. The registered plan provided only for abundance accounting or failing to account for the signal; reading the outcome as an artifact of matching is post hoc. Modeling the matched contrast shows what it tracks (post hoc; Supplemental Note 19): at fixed abundance, identification rises by about 1.08 in odds per cysteine, length adds nothing once cysteine count is fixed, and a null model built on abundance and cysteine count alone reproduces the within-decile contrasts. A protein-level comparison therefore needs abundance and cysteine count held fixed together, an unmatched comparison is not automatically conservative, and without a companion quantification, the usual case, it cannot be corrected in either direction.

## SR4. Artifact 4: coincidence of positive and identified sets (full text)

Main text: the section on steps that remove the comparison; Fig. 4a; Supplemental Notes 7 and 18.

Whether a site table can answer "why this cysteine" depends on how many identified cysteines carry no site assignment. In PXD048216 [38], an ABE study of *Ustilaginoidea virens*, 3,347 of 3,554 identified cysteines (94.18%; Wilson 95% interval 93.36–94.90%) carried a carbamidomethyl (CAM) site assignment, leaving little natural negative class (Supplemental Note 7). Placing the sites at random among each protein's cysteines gave 33.85%, which excludes the reading that the high share reflects which proteins were selected, although not differences in sequence coverage. Of the remaining 207 cysteines, 80 carried an N-ethylmaleimide (NEM) site alone. Against such a table, non-detected cysteines are not a valid within-sample negative control, and theoretical detectability does not repair it: the sequence rule included 3,523 of the 3,554 observed cysteines (99.13%) but had a positive predictive value of 26.58%, so it bounds what could have been seen rather than describing what was seen. Depletion alone does not identify the mechanism either: of the deposit's 652 multi-cysteine peptides, 401 (61.50%) carried both CAM and NEM, 241 (36.96%) CAM throughout and 10 neither.

The coincidence depends on the digest as well as on the enrichment. In the four-protease deposit the trypsin arm retains a large unmodified class, with coincidence 0.2551 [0.2380, 0.2724], whereas chymotrypsin, GluC and AspN give 0.5646, 0.5871 and 0.6150 (Fig. S1c). The registered plan had predicted depletion in the trypsin arm too, so the dependence on the digest is a post hoc reading; it persists when the arms are compared at the same protein-abundance distribution (Supplemental Note 18). Published cohorts whose tables report the identified-but-unassigned cysteines span most of the range: 0.0675 [0.0546, 0.0832] and 0.193 [0.1734, 0.2143] in two QTRP arms, 0.3588 [0.3381, 0.3799] in qPerS-SID and 0.8094 [0.7922, 0.8254] with the YAP1C reporter, whereas a capture-only FAT-switch site map reports no observed-unmodified class (post hoc; Supplemental Note 7). The share of identified cysteines carrying a site measures both enrichment success and the size of the natural negative class, so it should be reported routinely; a blocking arm or an unenriched aliquot makes the observed negative class available.

## SR5. Artifact 5: search space and near-isobaric chemistries (full text)

Main text: the section on steps that remove the comparison; Fig. 4b, c; Fig. S2; Supplemental Note 6.

Sulfide and cysteine dioxidation differ by 0.017758 Da [41], 7.1 ppm of a 2.5-kDa peptide. For an unbiased measurement a ±10-ppm precursor window admits both candidates for every peptide heavier than 1,776 Da, about two thirds of the theoretical tryptic cysteine peptides of the mouse proteome (post hoc; Fig. S2a). Whether the fragments can then decide depends on the MS2 resolution: in 1.0005-Da bins (ion-trap MS2) the fragments of the two candidates almost always share a bin, whereas in 0.02-Da bins (high-resolution MS2) the shift moves 89% of singly charged fragments containing the modified cysteine into another bin. A reported chemistry therefore reflects which candidates the search allowed and, where both were allowed, the calibrated precursor error and the fragments that contain the modified cysteine, none of which can be checked from a deposit that does not record them.

We examined this on the persulfidation deposit PXD015307 [42]. Its proteome-scale file reads persulfidation out through a trapping-and-tagging chemistry with iodoTMT, and the deposit's search placed no sulfide there; its four other files carry deposit-side identifications of insulin chains only (53 PSMs), four of them with sulfide, and the consensus files record neither the searched modifications nor the precursor tolerance. Re-searching the five raw files with both candidates in the search space returned no +32-class cysteine identification at 1% FDR in any file (245 PSMs in the proteome-scale file, 14 to 26 in each insulin file), the underpowered branch registered before the search, so the deposit supports no rate for either chemistry (Fig. S2c; Supplemental Note 6). The re-search could not re-examine the deposit's four sulfide PSMs, which lie on human insulin chains and three of which carry NEM, which was not searched, and their own precursor errors do not decide them (post hoc; Fig. S2b): the deposit's A-chain PSMs without sulfide sit 6.4 ppm below its B-chain PSMs, comparable to the 7.0-ppm separation, so the reading of an A-chain assignment depends on which PSMs define the baseline. Of the four, one B-chain PSM lies at the sulfide expectation under all eight baselines examined, the other fits either chemistry, one A-chain PSM reads as sulfide or as dioxidation depending on the baseline and the other fits neither. Score ties follow the fragment binning (post hoc; Fig. S2d): among single-cysteine spectra with both candidates inside the window, 83% tied at the best score with 1.0005-Da bins against 30% with 0.02-Da bins.

Artifact 5 therefore precludes a conclusion rather than distorting one: a chemical identity that neither the search space, the precursor mass nor the fragments could have decided is an assignment rather than a measurement, and without a record of the search the two cannot be told apart. The check (Table 1, item 8) lists the modifications searched, the precursor tolerance and the fragment resolution, and expresses each near-isobaric separation in ppm against the run's own precursor-error distribution; it does not apply where the identity is inferred from the enrichment chemistry. The same arithmetic applies to phosphorylation and sulfation, which differ by 0.009515 Da. Of eight phosphoproteomics deposits returned by one registered PRIDE query, two exceeded a file-size cap and two had not searched phosphorylation; none of the other four had sulfation in its search space, and had it been searched, their 4.5-ppm main-search window would have admitted both candidates for the 21% to 48% of identified phosphopeptides heavier than 2,115 Da (PXD071110 and PXD071012 [80]; PXD068672 and PXD068395 [83]; Source Data). This check uses no fragment evidence and no measured mass errors, so it shows what the searches could not report, not that any phosphopeptide was misassigned, which a repository-wide screen found to be rare in high-accuracy data [59].

## SR6. The planted-artifact benchmark (full text)

Main text: the section on the checklist and tool; Fig. S6; Supplemental Notes 3 and 17.

A diagnostic is only as good as its response to a planted signal. In four registered synthetic benchmarks, one for each mechanism that can be simulated end to end, each artifact was injected at six strengths with 50 replicates per point, 1,200 audited datasets in all (Supplemental Note 3). Because each statistic is the quantity the injection manipulates, the benchmarks measure calibration and power against a known truth; separating a measurement artifact from a genuine chemical preference is the task of the empirical controls above. Detection rose with injected strength in all four benchmarks and reached every replicate by strength 0.5, with AUROC 0.9568 to 0.996 and AUPRC 0.9915 to 0.9992 between artifact-free and artifact-bearing datasets, against a no-skill AUPRC of 0.8333 (Fig. S6a, b). The registered Spearman correlation between strength and detection was 1.0 in all four, or 0.8452 to 0.9411 when recomputed post hoc with average ranks for saturated values. At strength 0.1, detection ranged from 0.22 (cleavage) to 0.88 (abundance). At strength zero, where the correct answer is silence, the flag rate was 0.02 to 0.14, and two benchmarks exceeded the registered limit of 0.1 (Fig. S6c), so they did not meet the registered pass rule. Extending their strength-zero runs after that failure (post hoc) gave 21 of 300 (0.07; Wilson 95% interval 0.0462 to 0.1046) and 11 of 200 (0.055; 0.031 to 0.0958), and 10,000 newly generated datasets of the cleavage benchmark gave 0.051 [0.047, 0.056] (Supplemental Note 17), consistent with the nominal 0.05 of the interval test.

## SR7. Re-testing published claims (full text)

Main text: the section on re-testing 28 published claims; Fig. 5, Fig. S4 and Fig. S5; Supplemental Note 14. In the main text the ten protein-level claims are reported as not estimable (Table 1, item 6) and do not enter the count of surviving claims; their re-test verdicts are given here and in Supplemental Data 2. Of the ten site-level undecidable claims, eight (`PERS-003`, `SFE-007`, `SNO-001`, `SNO-002`, `SNO-005`, `SNO-009`, `SNO-012`, `SNO-014`) had a reconstructed baseline interval covering zero before any control was applied; the other two are `PERS-002` and `SFE-008` (Table S14.1). The history of the re-test tool version is recorded in Supplemental Note 14 and Supplemental Data 12.

Three design choices determine whether a site-preference claim is testable: how the negative set is constructed, how the background is chosen and how precisely the attribute is defined (Fig. 1b; Fig. 2 and Fig. S5). We re-tested published claims along these axes. Of 51 claims extracted from 23 papers across four cysteine switch chemistries [1–10,46,60–71], 35 entered the re-test and 28 have a measured baseline (Supplemental Data 1 and 2). Eleven of these baselines were reproduced on the authors' own terms; the rest required a recorded substitution of the attribute, the negative set, the background or the cohort, so an undecidable verdict can reflect either a small effect or an incomplete reconstruction, and the per-claim reproduction status accompanies every row of Fig. 5a and Fig. S4 (Source Data).

The counts in this section cover all 28 claims with a measured baseline, including the ten protein-level claims that the main text reports as not estimable (Table 1, item 6); the main text's site-level counts are 4 survives, 2 attenuated, 10 undecidable, 1 null broken by control and 1 baseline contradicts claim. Of the 28 claims, 11 survive the matched control, 2 are attenuated, 13 are undecidable, 1 is an effect that only the control reveals and 1 has a baseline running against the published direction, in a transfer cohort; no directional claim reverses and none vanishes on its authors' own data (Fig. 5a and Fig. S4; Supplemental Note 14). The control itself produced few of the undecidable verdicts: in 11 of the 13 the reconstructed baseline interval already covered zero before any control was applied, and only one claim moved from a baseline effect to undecidable because of the control. What limits these claims is testability with the material their papers provide. Ten of the 28 are protein-level enrichment claims, of which 7 survive; the main text does not count these as surviving; but by this paper's own checklist (Table 1, item 6) protein-level commonality is not estimable without a protein-level negative class, which none of these cohorts provides, so those seven are better read as not estimable than as supported. Among the 18 site-level claims, 4 survive, 2 are attenuated, 10 are undecidable, 1 is an effect revealed by the control and 1 a baseline running against the published direction in a transfer cohort. The 28 claims come from 15 papers and 20 site tables, one paper contributing 7 (Supplemental Data 10). Four were re-tested only on a substitute cohort, so statements about the authors' own data cover the other 24.

The control acted on some attributes more than on others. On the authors' own data, the three basic-residue motifs kept 0.37 to 0.55 of their baseline effect (`PERS-009`, `SFE-006`, `SNO-021`), whereas the two acidic motifs kept 0.83 and 1.02 (`SFE-002`, `SNO-006`) and the accessibility claim `SFE-001` kept 0.89; the same-size random control kept 0.91 to 1.02 in every class (post hoc; Supplemental Note 14). This is the direction Artifact 1 predicts. In simulations on real sequences with the observed detection, matching removed only 3 to 23% of a genuine effect planted on the attributes as the claims define them, beyond what a sequence-independent attribute loses (post hoc; Supplemental Note 15); but because the matching covariates are computed from lysine and arginine positions, the loss in the basic motifs cannot by itself be told apart from a genuine chemistry that depends on nearby basic residues, and the pattern rests on three claims against two.

`SFE-006` [6] shows what the random control adds. On the Yang 2014 component of the authors' cohort [62] (9,240 sites, 1,095 positives, 776 proteins) the baseline association is log2 OR 1.3840 (Fisher P = 3.85e-29, uncorrected); after detectability matching it falls to 0.5950, while the same-size random control sits at 1.3918, 100.6% of the baseline, so the loss is due to the control, not to the smaller sample, and the claim is attenuated, not absent (it vanishes only in a transfer cohort).

**Background choice.** The authors of `PERS-008` [65] tested zinc-finger persulfidation against the zinc-finger complement of each species, with the whole proteome as a check. Against their primary background, the 474 zinc-finger proteins eligible for the claim with 118 positives, the baseline is 0.0727 [−0.9228, 0.9198], both controls cross zero and the claim is undecidable (Fig. S5a). Against the whole proteome the same claim looks strong and survives the control: baseline log2 OR 3.4971 [2.5466, 4.1757], matched 1.9300 [0.5408, 3.9806] and same-size random control 3.1041 across 54,855 proteins (Fig. 5a and Fig. S4). We report the authors' own background as primary; choosing the wider one converts an undecidable claim into one that survives.

**Attribute definition.** Five structural claims share one feature table, instrument and control, and separate by how precisely the original paper stated its attribute (Fig. S5b; Supplemental Note 8). `SFE-001` [62] states both quantity and threshold (RSA > 25%) and reproduces by a different method: Shrake–Rupley accessibility [32] on AlphaFold models gives 66.3% / 24.9% where the authors, using a sequence predictor, reported >60% / <30%. It survives the control with 0.886 of its baseline retained (0.946 under pLDDT ≥ 70). `SNO-002` [63], re-tested under its stated 10% relative-accessibility cutoff, is undecidable in every specification tried (baseline 0.0539 [−0.3940, 0.4798]): its composition rebuilds to 34.0% of modified and 33.2% of unmodified cysteines exposed, against the 29% and 23% the authors report. `SNO-012`, re-tested under its stated 1.0 Å² sulfur-atom criterion, strengthens under the control (matched 1.7482 [0.5284, 3.2156]), but its primary baseline interval covers zero, so it is undecidable in the primary specification and survives under pLDDT ≥ 70. `SNO-001`'s categories, assigned with DSSP [87] in the original study, are not reproducible under P-SEA and `SNO-009`'s per-position statistic does not reproduce; both are undecidable, which is not the same as refuted.

**Effects that the control does not explain.** `PERS-002` [66] reported no preference, but the reconstructed baseline is a significant deficit, −1.6857 [−2.5687, −0.9847], which matching (−1.6026) and the random control (−1.6445) barely move (724 positives, 1,294 negatives). A deficit is compatible with the authors' statement that such motifs are not a preferential target, but not with the absence of preference that the claim was coded as, so the reading rule records it as undecidable; the deficit is not explained by detectability.

## SR8. The negative set and detectability-only features (full text)

Main text: the first Results section; Fig. 2; Supplemental Note 20.

The same published site table can be scored against two negative sets (Fig. 2; absolute values and ratios in Supplemental Note 20). Against the unmatched set (NEG_A, every other cysteine of a protein carrying a site), the ten visibility features alone separated sites from the other cysteines with global AUC 0.711–0.855 in seven cohorts and 0.64 [0.56, 0.72] in QTRP S2 (80 sites), and with within-protein AUC 0.66–0.88; the 25 digest features reached 0.73–0.93 globally and 0.72–0.94 within proteins. Against the observed set (NEG_B, the cysteines the same experiment reported unmodified), VIS10 fell to 0.53–0.62 globally and 0.54–0.65 within proteins in five of the seven cohorts that provide one, and to 0.43 [0.37, 0.50] in QTRP S2, as the construction predicts once both classes consist of detected cysteines. With the YAP1C reporter, whose positives were detected as cross-linked peptides and negatives as linear peptides, VIS10 did not fall (0.77 [0.75, 0.80]). DIG25 still separated detected sites from detected unmodified cysteines of the same protein, with within-protein AUC 0.63–0.84 in six cohorts, every interval above 0.5, and 0.53 [0.40, 0.68] in QTRP S2; the FAT-switch cohort reports no observed-unmodified class.

Two readings follow. Against an unmatched negative set, descriptors of the theoretical tryptic peptide alone separate sites from the other cysteines of the same proteins, so a sequence or structural feature tested against that background must add discrimination beyond them before it is read as chemistry. An observed-unmodified class removes much of the visibility contrast, but not the discrimination carried by the remaining digest columns, and observed does not mean equally observable. These are comparisons between arms within a cohort, not prevalence estimates; every cohort is scored under the tryptic rule and the models are not refit within bootstrap replicates.

## SR9. Detectability-only features and a published site predictor (full text)

Main text: the section on the published predictor; Fig. 6a; Supplemental Notes 5 and 22. The recovered shares below are the ratios of Table S22.1; the main text reports the paired differences of the same table.

The pLMSNOSite S-nitrosylation benchmark [47] fixes a training set (3,276 sites and as many other cysteines, 1,918 proteins) and a held-out test set (351 sites and 3,168 other cysteines of 278 proteins, 4 of them shared with training). Trained on the training set with the fixed configuration of the Fig. 2 analysis and no tuning, the ten visibility features alone reached a test AUROC of 0.7095 [0.6817, 0.7367], and all 25 detectability features 0.7765 [0.7231, 0.8274]; a model trained on permuted labels gave 0.5124 [0.471, 0.5546]. The AUROC tabulated for pLMSNOSite, 0.754, is computed by its authors' evaluation script from predictions thresholded at 0.5, so it is the mean of sensitivity and specificity at one threshold rather than a ranking statistic. We therefore ran the released models on the released test set (post hoc; Supplemental Note 22). Scored on its probabilities, pLMSNOSite reaches a test AUROC of 0.815 [0.763, 0.858], and the 25 detectability features recover 0.88 [0.69, 1.08] of its above-chance AUROC across test sites. Within proteins, over the 220 test proteins that carry both labels, the ten visibility features reach 0.690 against pLMSNOSite's 0.702, recovering 0.94 [0.71, 1.24] of its above-chance within-protein AUROC, and the 25 features 0.91 [0.70, 1.16]; neither paired difference is resolved. The registered denominator was the tabulated 0.754, and its replacement by the re-scored value is declared here.

Three cautions bound the reading. The comparison is confined to AUROC: at this prevalence (0.0997), precision–recall leaves much less room for a detectability model (Supplemental Note 22). The 25-feature set includes the number of lysines and arginines near the cysteine, which could also carry a chemical acid–base motif, so the ten-feature figure is the conservative one. And the benchmark's negatives are other cysteines of S-nitrosylated proteins, an unmatched background of the kind examined above. The result does not make a detectability model a better predictor; it shows that on this benchmark one cannot be told apart from the published predictor when sites are ranked within proteins.

## SR10. Self-audit of our own ranker (full text)

Main text: the section on the published predictor (second paragraph); Fig. 6b and Fig. S3; Supplemental Notes 2 and 9–12, 21.

We applied the same instruments to a persulfidation site ranker that we had built, a single LightGBM model trained on one public cohort and applied zero-shot to the other, in both directions: human PXD044043, with 18,567 cysteines, 3,206 of them labeled, in 1,519 proteins, and rice PXD072089, with 5,004 cysteines, 737 labeled, in 581 proteins (Supplemental Note 2). The labels of both cohorts are T3 in the label standard below, so neither can support a statement about chemical propensity as distinct from annotation ranking. Within each label stratum, where the label is constant, the score still ranked cysteines by the distal 6–12-residue trypsin band: AUC 0.6298 [0.5733, 0.6858] among label-positive and 0.6474 [0.6255, 0.6693] among label-negative rice sites, and 0.5906 [0.5559, 0.6231] and 0.5813 [0.5693, 0.5931] in human (Fig. S3a). Permuting scores 1,000 times within each stratum gave null ranges covering 0.5 in all eight cells (0.4436 to 0.5573), against which every observed AUC is significant at the permutation floor (p < 0.002); permuting within proteins gave 0.4883 to 0.5336, so a small part of the association reflects the ordering of proteins (Supplemental Note 9). The ordering can also reflect how the labels were assigned, because only the sole cysteine of an identified peptide can be labeled (Supplemental Note 21). Under five cleavage rules, the label-positive distal AUC was highest for trypsin in both directions; LysC gave 0.6051 [0.5611, 0.6464] in rice but 0.5086 [0.4873, 0.5302] in human, and GluC, AspN and chymotrypsin stayed close to 0.5 (0.4678 to 0.5277 in rice, 0.4785 to 0.5118 in human), although no test between proteases was pre-specified and the trypsin and LysC intervals overlap in rice (Fig. S3d; Supplemental Note 10).

Excluding visibility features by design does not prevent this. The ranker's chemistry set declares no visibility feature but includes basic-residue composition in windows out to 50 residues, through which the distal association remains reachable. In a three-arm ablation refit within each cohort (Table S1; Supplemental Note 11), every top-100 distal log2 odds ratio stayed at or above the level that label inheritance alone produces, about 1.2 in human and 0.76 in rice (post hoc; Supplemental Note 21), while the point estimate fell in rice (2.486 to 1.6294) and rose in human (1.7343 to 2.8961) as the named columns were deleted; the arms' intervals overlap, and deleting all twenty columns reduced the within-protein AUC by 0.009 [0.0005, 0.017] in human and by nothing detectable in rice (paired test, post hoc). Conversely, a 25-feature set containing no chemistry at all, read through the LightGBM ranking member that sees only its own columns, reached within-protein AUC 0.8819 against the full 1,107-column model's 0.8884 on the human cohort, recovering 0.9832 [0.9655, 1.0016] of its above-chance discrimination, and 0.8023 against 0.901 on rice (0.7538 [0.7036, 0.8049]; Fig. S3c; Supplemental Note 12). The registered reading was the three-member blend, one member of which also receives a protein language-model embedding [55]; through the blend the ratios are 0.9952 [0.9779, 1.0133] and 0.8527 [0.8069, 0.9], and the choice of member to report is post hoc. A high recovered share is what labels close to "was this residue reported" predict: detectability is not a contaminant in this task but most of the task, and control has to be built into the evaluation design rather than the feature list.

## SR11. The literature survey (full text)

Main text: the section on the checklist and tool; Fig. 1c and Fig. S7; Supplemental Data 9 and 11.

An exploratory survey asked how often site-level modification analyses in general report a matched background (Fig. S7 and Fig. 1c). The 74 classifiable papers comprise 11 on cysteine redox modifications, 3 on S-palmitoylation, 24 on phosphorylation, 12 on acetylation, 12 on other lysine acylations, 4 on ubiquitination, 5 on several modifications and 3 others (Supplemental Data 9). None was coded as matching its background on theoretical detectability or abundance, and 2 (2.7%, 95% CI 0.7–9.3), both S-palmitoylation studies, used residues detected but unassigned in the same experiment; 35 (47.3%, 36.3–58.5) compared against all residues of the relevant type, and 37 (50.0%, 38.9–61.1) fell in a residual class covering both backgrounds we could not classify and backgrounds not stated. One paper discussed cleavage or detectability bias and two reported the fraction of identified residues carrying a site assignment. The coding was performed by a language model, and its blinded re-coding agreed only moderately with the original codes (κ 0.31; Experimental Procedures); three records originally coded as matched did not survive re-coding and re-reading of the full text (Supplemental Data 11). We therefore read these proportions as indicating that matched backgrounds are rarely reported, not as prevalence estimates; because the queries favor papers that discuss background sets, 2.7% is more likely to overestimate than to underestimate their use.

## SR12. How coarse the theoretical detectability model is

Main text: Discussion, final paragraph; Supplemental Methods (Empirical detectability check).

A registered check on the unenriched arm of the four-protease deposit shows how coarse that model is: among 264,782 cysteine-containing tryptic candidates, the theoretical flag alone separated the 6,658 identified peptides from the rest with an AUC of 0.5718, whereas an empirical model adding protein abundance measured in the same run, sequence composition, distance to the protein termini and identification in the other arms reached 0.9349, with abundance the largest coefficient. Theoretical matching therefore removes what could never be seen, not what was merely unlikely to be seen, and abundance-aware or experimentally observed backgrounds are the stronger control where they exist.
